When to premedicate — prior allergic-like reaction to the same class of contrast (iodinated→iodinated, gadolinium→gadolinium). NOT indicated for shellfish/seafood, topical iodine, asthma alone, or reactions to a different class.
Adult — Elective (12–13 h Oral)
Prednisone Regimen
  • Prednisone 50 mg PO at 13 h, 7 h, and 1 h pre-contrast
  • + Diphenhydramine 50 mg PO/IM/IV at 1 h pre-contrast
If unable to take PO: substitute hydrocortisone 200 mg IV for each oral prednisone dose.
Methylprednisolone Regimen
  • Methylprednisolone 32 mg PO at 12 h and 2 h pre-contrast
  • ± Diphenhydramine 50 mg at 1 h pre-contrast
Adult — Accelerated IV (Urgent, 4–5 h)
  1. Methylprednisolone Na succinate 40 mg IV or Hydrocortisone Na succinate 200 mg IV immediately, then every 4 h until contrast
    + Diphenhydramine 50 mg IV 1 h pre-contrast
  2. Dexamethasone Na sulfate 7.5 mg IV immediately, then every 4 h
    + Diphenhydramine 50 mg IV 1 h pre-contrast
  3. Same drugs each 1 h pre-contrast — <4 h regimens have NO evidence of efficacy; only in true emergencies.
Paediatric (ACR Tables A/B)
Elective (Oral)
  • Prednisone 0.5–0.7 mg/kg PO (max 50 mg) at 13 h / 7 h / 1 h
  • + Diphenhydramine 1.25 mg/kg PO (max 50 mg) at 1 h
Urgent / ED / NPO (IV)
  • Hydrocortisone 2 mg/kg IV (max 200 mg) at 5 h and 1 h
  • + Diphenhydramine 1 mg/kg IV (max 50 mg) at 1 h

NOT indicated for premedication: shellfish/seafood allergy · topical iodine (povidone-iodine) allergy · isolated asthma · reaction to a different class of contrast. Iodinated↔gadolinium cross-reactivity is NOT established.

Hives / Urticaria Mild
  • Stop or slow injection if active.
  • Reassure; observe.
  • If symptomatic / progressing: Diphenhydramine 25–50 mg PO/IM/IV (slow over 1–2 min) Diphenhydramine 1 mg/kg PO/IM/IV (max 50 mg)
  • Watch for progression to bronchospasm / laryngeal oedema.
Bronchospasm Mild → Severe
  • O₂ 6–10 L/min by mask.
  • Mild: Salbutamol/Albuterol MDI 2 puffs × up to 3.
  • Moderate / severe (no response): Epinephrine IM 0.3 mg (1:1,000) lateral thigh — repeat q5–15 min Epinephrine IM 0.01 mg/kg (1:1,000), max single 0.3 mg
  • Call emergency response team.
Hypotension + Bradycardia Vasovagal
  • Legs elevated (Trendelenburg).
  • O₂ 6–10 L/min by mask.
  • IV fluids: 0.9% saline 500–1000 mL rapid bolus 0.9% saline 10–20 mL/kg bolus (max 500–1000 mL)
  • If persistent / symptomatic: Atropine 0.6–1 mg IV, may repeat to 3 mg total Atropine 0.02 mg/kg IV (min 0.1 mg, max 0.6 mg)
Laryngeal Oedema Severe
  • O₂ 6–10 L/min by mask.
  • Epinephrine — first line: IM 0.3 mg (1:1,000) lateral thigh — repeat q5–15 min IM 0.01 mg/kg (1:1,000), max 0.3 mg single dose
  • Call emergency response team / anaesthesia / ENT.
  • Prepare for airway management.
Anaphylaxis Life-threatening
  • Call resus / 999 immediately.
  • O₂ 6–10 L/min by mask. Legs elevated.
  • Epinephrine FIRST line: IM 0.3 mg (1:1,000) lateral thigh — repeat q5–15 min, max 1 mg total
    IV (with monitoring): 0.1 mg slow over 1–2 min from 1:10,000     IM 0.01 mg/kg (1:1,000), max single 0.3 mg, repeat q5–15 min
    Auto-injector: EpiPen Jr 0.15 mg if <30 kg · EpiPen 0.3 mg if ≥30 kg
  • IV fluids — large-volume rapid bolus: 0.9% saline 1 L rapid; repeat as needed 0.9% saline 10–20 mL/kg rapid; repeat
  • Diphenhydramine and steroids are adjuncts only — never delay epinephrine.

Extravasation rate ~0.3–0.7% with peripheral IV iodinated contrast. Most resolve without sequelae; severe complications (compartment syndrome, ulceration) are triggered by clinical signs, not by extravasated volume alone.
Initial Management
  • STOP injection immediately.
  • Elevate the affected limb above heart level.
  • Apply cold OR warm compresses (either acceptable; relieve symptoms).
  • Document: site, estimated volume, contrast type, time, immediate clinical signs.
  • Reassure the patient and explain follow-up.
Urgent Surgical / Plastics Consult — based on signs, NOT volume
Obtain urgent review if any of the following develop:
  • Severe or progressive pain.
  • Progressive swelling or tense compartment.
  • Decreased capillary refill or altered perfusion.
  • Altered sensation or paraesthesia.
  • Worsening active or passive range of motion.
  • Skin blistering or ulceration.
Most common severe complication: compartment syndrome.
NOT recommended: hyaluronidase · steroids · anticoagulants · using a fixed volume threshold (e.g. 100–150 mL) as the sole trigger for surgical consultation.
Outpatient Discharge
Provide written instructions to return for worsening pain, paraesthesia, reduced movement, or skin changes. Severe injury may evolve over hours.
ACR 2025 single threshold: eGFR 30 mL/min/1.73 m². Above 30, IV iodinated contrast is not an independent nephrotoxic risk factor (level-1 evidence, Davenport / McDonald / Hinson 2013–2014 propensity-matched studies).
eGFR Thresholds
eGFR ≥ 45
Standard dose contrast — no prophylaxis required.
eGFR 30 – 44
Borderline; not a statistically significant risk. Give contrast at standard dose if clinically indicated.
eGFR < 30 or AKI
Weigh risks/benefits. Consider IV 0.9% saline prophylaxis. Not an absolute contraindication.
Metformin (ACR Categories)
Category I — eGFR ≥ 30 & no AKI
  • Continue metformin normally.
  • No need to recheck renal function after contrast.
Category II — eGFR < 30, AKI, or intra-arterial study
  • Stop metformin at the time of the procedure.
  • Withhold for 48 h.
  • Restart only after renal function rechecked & confirmed normal.
Gadolinium at standard MRI dose (0.1–0.3 mmol/kg): no metformin interruption needed.
Gadolinium-Based Contrast (GBCA) & NSF Risk
Group I — Greatest NSF risk
Contraindicated if eGFR <30, AKI or dialysis.
  • Gadodiamide (Omniscan)
  • Gadopentetate (Magnevist)
  • Gadoversetamide (OptiMARK)
Group II — Few/no unconfounded NSF cases
Safe at standard dose in CKD/AKI/dialysis. eGFR screening optional.
  • Gadobutrol (Gadavist/Gadovist)
  • Gadoteric acid (Dotarem/Clariscan)
  • Gadoteridol (ProHance)
  • Gadobenate (MultiHance)
  • Gadopiclenol (Elucirem/Vueway)
  • Gadoxetate (Eovist/Primovist)
Group III
No agents currently classified (Apr 2024).
UK practice: macrocyclic Group II agents (Dotarem / Gadavist / ProHance) are standard; linear Group I agents are largely withdrawn (EMA 2017).
NOT recommended (per ACR 2025): N-acetylcysteine for CI-AKI prevention · sodium bicarbonate (no better than saline) · contrast dose reduction in high-risk patients (produces non-diagnostic scans without changing risk) · diuretics or mannitol prophylaxis · routine baseline creatinine in patients without renal risk factors.
Pregnancy
  • Iodinated contrast — acceptable if the indication is warranted; the benefit of timely diagnosis usually outweighs theoretical fetal risk.
  • Gadolinium (GBCA) — only if benefit substantially outweighs the (uncertain) fetal risk; prefer non-contrast MRI when possible.
  • Discuss with the referrer; document risk/benefit conversation in the report.
Breast-Feeding
  • <0.01% of maternal IV iodinated contrast dose reaches breast milk; <1% of that is absorbed by the infant gut.
  • <0.04% of maternal GBCA dose reaches breast milk; systemic infant dose <0.0004%.
  • Continue breast-feeding without interruption after maternal iodinated or GBCA contrast.
  • A 12–24 h pause is optional at maternal preference only; no medical benefit beyond 24 h.
  • Routine neonatal thyroid function testing is NOT required.
Thyroid Disease
  • Hyperthyroidism alone is not a contraindication and does not require premedication.
  • Acute thyroid storm: avoid iodinated contrast — can potentiate thyrotoxicosis. Steroid premedication will not help.
  • Before radioactive iodine therapy or imaging — washout after iodinated contrast:
    • 3–4 weeks for hyperthyroidism
    • 6 weeks for hypothyroidism
  • A single maternal dose of iodinated contrast in pregnancy has no effect on neonatal thyroid function.
  • Iodinated contrast does not affect TFTs in patients with normal thyroid function.
NOT contraindications / NOT cross-reactive: shellfish or seafood allergy · topical iodine (povidone-iodine) allergy · isolated asthma. These should never be used to justify withholding contrast or premedication.

Key Imaging Tips

Arterial Phase Enhancement

HCC typically shows arterial hyperenhancement due to arterial blood supply

Look for early arterial enhancement at 20-30 seconds post-contrast. This is the hallmark of HCC diagnosis.

Portal Venous Washout

Characteristic washout in portal venous and delayed phases

HCC becomes hypodense relative to liver parenchyma in portal venous phase (60-70 seconds) due to lack of portal venous supply.

Vascular Invasion Assessment

Critical for T2 vs T1b differentiation in solitary tumors

Look for tumor thrombus in portal or hepatic veins, vessel encasement, or irregular vessel contours. Use coronal/sagittal reformats.

Tumor Counting Technique

Systematic approach to count all viable tumor nodules

Review all phases systematically. Count enhancing nodules in arterial phase. Exclude treated lesions showing no enhancement.

Size Measurement Standards

Measure maximum dimension in any imaging plane

Use the largest measurement from axial, coronal, or sagittal planes. Measure enhancing component only, excluding necrotic areas.

Capsule vs Pseudocapsule

Distinguish true capsular invasion from pseudocapsule

Pseudocapsule appears as delayed enhancement rim. True capsular invasion shows irregular margins and loss of capsular enhancement.

Adjacent Organ Invasion

Assess for T4 features - direct organ invasion

Look for loss of fat planes, irregular interfaces with diaphragm, adrenal, colon. Gallbladder invasion doesn't qualify for T4.

Regional Lymph Node Assessment

Focus on porta hepatis, periceliac, and portacaval regions

Size criteria >1cm short axis. Look for abnormal enhancement patterns. Fibrolamellar HCC has higher propensity for nodal involvement.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Satellite Nodules with Separate Tumors

Satellite nodules within 2cm of main tumor are part of local extension, not separate tumors. Only count discrete, separate enhancing nodules as multiple tumors for T staging.

2
Missing Microscopic Vascular Invasion

Microscopic vascular invasion may not be visible on imaging but affects T staging. If pathology reports vascular invasion in a solitary >2cm tumor, it's T2, not T1b.

3
Gallbladder Invasion Misclassification

Direct gallbladder invasion does NOT qualify for T4 staging. Only invasion of other adjacent organs (diaphragm, adrenal, colon) qualifies for T4.

4
Overcalling Portal Vein Invasion

Distinguish between tumor thrombus (T4) and external compression (not T4). True invasion shows intraluminal enhancing material continuous with tumor.

5
Ignoring Treated Lesions

Don't count treated lesions showing complete response (no enhancement) in tumor burden assessment. Only count viable, enhancing tumor tissue.

6
Misinterpreting Regenerative Nodules

In cirrhotic liver, distinguish HCC from regenerative/dysplastic nodules. HCC shows arterial enhancement with washout; regenerative nodules typically don't.

7
Inadequate Phase Timing

Poor arterial phase timing can miss HCC or lead to misdiagnosis. Ensure proper bolus tracking and optimal arterial phase at 20-30 seconds post-contrast.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Assess Liver Background

Evaluate for cirrhosis, portal hypertension signs (splenomegaly, ascites, varices). This affects prognosis and treatment options beyond TNM staging.

2
Count All Tumors

Systematically review all phases to identify and count all viable tumor nodules. Use arterial phase for detection, confirm with other phases.

3
Measure Largest Tumor

Measure maximum dimension of largest tumor in any plane. If multiple tumors, also note the size of the largest among them.

4
Assess Vascular Invasion

Examine portal and hepatic veins for tumor thrombus. Look for vessel encasement, irregular contours, or intraluminal enhancing material.

5
Evaluate Adjacent Organs

Check for direct invasion of diaphragm, adrenal glands, colon, or other adjacent structures. Assess for peritoneal perforation.

6
Examine Regional Lymph Nodes

Focus on porta hepatis, periceliac, and portacaval regions. Apply size criteria (>1cm short axis) and enhancement patterns.

7
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate lungs, bones, adrenals, and other organs for metastatic disease. Include assessment of peritoneal deposits.

8
Integrate Findings for Final Stage

Combine T, N, M assessments using AJCC criteria. Consider clinical implications and treatment options based on final stage.

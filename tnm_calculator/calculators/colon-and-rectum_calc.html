Key Imaging Tips

T3 Substaging in Rectal Cancer

Measure extramural depth on high-resolution T2-weighted MRI

T3a: ≤1mm, T3b: 1-5mm, T3c: 5-15mm, T3d: >15mm beyond muscularis propria

Peritoneal Reflection Assessment

Critical for determining T4a vs T3 staging

Upper rectum: covered by peritoneum anteriorly and laterally. Middle rectum: anterior peritoneum only. Lower rectum: no peritoneal covering

Tumor Deposits Recognition

Discrete tumor foci in fat without lymph node architecture

Look for irregular, spiculated soft tissue nodules >3mm in pericolic/perirectal fat. If lymph node architecture visible = nodal metastasis

Extramural Vascular Invasion (EMVI)

Important prognostic factor in rectal cancer

Tumor signal within vessels beyond muscularis propria on T2W MRI. Appears as intermediate signal expanding vessel contour

Circumferential Resection Margin (CRM)

Measure shortest distance from tumor to mesorectal fascia

CRM ≤1mm = involved margin (poor prognosis). Measure on axial T2W MRI perpendicular to rectal wall

Liver Metastasis Detection

Use portal venous phase CT or hepatobiliary MRI

Small metastases best seen on portal venous phase. DWI and hepatobiliary agents increase sensitivity for small lesions

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing T4a with T3

T4a requires visceral peritoneum invasion, not just reaching the serosa. Look for actual penetration through the peritoneal surface.

2
Missing tumor deposits

Tumor deposits can be subtle and are often overlooked. Systematically search pericolic/perirectal fat for irregular soft tissue nodules.

3
Incorrect N1c staging

N1c requires tumor deposits WITHOUT nodal metastasis. If both present, stage by nodal count (N1a/b or N2a/b).

4
Overstaging inflammatory changes

Post-radiation fibrosis and inflammation can mimic tumor invasion. Use DWI and compare with pre-treatment imaging.

5
Ignoring peritoneal reflection anatomy

Peritoneal reflection varies by rectal level. Upper rectum has anterior/lateral peritoneum, middle has anterior only, lower has none.

6
Inadequate lymph node assessment

Minimum 12 lymph nodes required for adequate staging. Small nodes can harbor metastases - don't rely solely on size criteria.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor Location

Determine if colon or rectum. For rectum, identify level relative to peritoneal reflection and anal verge distance.

2
Assess Tumor Size and Morphology

Measure maximum dimensions, assess growth pattern (polypoid, ulcerative, infiltrative), and degree of bowel wall thickening.

3
Determine Depth of Invasion (T stage)

Systematically assess each bowel wall layer: mucosa → submucosa → muscularis propria → serosa/adventitia → adjacent structures.

4
Evaluate Regional Lymph Nodes

Count and measure all visible nodes. Assess for morphologic features of metastasis (size, shape, enhancement pattern).

5
Search for Tumor Deposits

Systematically examine pericolic/perirectal fat for discrete tumor foci without identifiable lymph node architecture.

6
Assess for Distant Metastases

Examine liver, lungs, peritoneum, and non-regional lymph nodes. Include ovaries in women and consider drop metastases.

7
Evaluate Prognostic Factors (Rectal)

For rectal cancer: measure CRM, assess EMVI, evaluate response to neoadjuvant therapy if applicable.

8
Synthesize Findings and Stage

Combine T, N, M components for overall stage. Consider clinical context and treatment implications in final assessment.

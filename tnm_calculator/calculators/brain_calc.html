Important Note

Traditional TNM staging is NOT routinely used for most CNS tumors. The AJCC 9th Edition only provides M staging for medulloepithelioma based on Chang criteria. For most brain and spinal cord tumors, WHO grading and molecular classification are more clinically relevant than TNM staging.

Critical Understanding

TNM Staging Limitations in CNS Tumors

The AJCC 9th Edition explicitly states that traditional TNM staging is NOT practical or relevant for most CNS tumors. The reasons include:

  • Tumor size is less relevant than location, histology, grade, and molecular characteristics
  • Brain and spinal cord have no intrinsic lymph nodes (N category not applicable)
  • Most CNS tumors favor local recurrence over distant metastasis
  • WHO grading and molecular classification are more clinically meaningful

Key Imaging Tips

CSF Seeding Assessment

Look for nodular enhancement along ventricular walls and spinal subarachnoid space

Use post-contrast T1-weighted images and FLAIR sequences for optimal detection

Spinal Imaging Protocol

Complete spine MRI is essential for staging medulloepithelioma and other high-grade tumors

Include sagittal and axial T1 post-contrast and T2-weighted sequences

Molecular Imaging Correlation

MRI features can suggest molecular subtypes in gliomas

IDH-mutant gliomas often show more homogeneous enhancement and less necrosis

Ventricular System Assessment

Carefully evaluate all ventricles for ependymal enhancement or nodularity

Use thin-section post-contrast T1-weighted images through the ventricular system

Meningeal Enhancement

Distinguish between reactive and neoplastic meningeal enhancement

Nodular enhancement is more concerning than smooth, thin enhancement

Systemic Staging

Consider chest CT and bone marrow biopsy for high-grade tumors with suspected systemic spread

Particularly important for medulloepithelioma and other embryonal tumors

Perfusion Imaging

DSC or ASL perfusion can help differentiate tumor grades

Higher-grade tumors typically show increased relative cerebral blood volume

Diffusion Characteristics

ADC values can provide information about tumor cellularity and grade

High-grade tumors often show restricted diffusion in cellular areas

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Applying TNM staging to all CNS tumors

TNM staging is only applicable to medulloepithelioma for M staging. Most CNS tumors use WHO grading instead. Always check if TNM staging is appropriate for the specific tumor type.

2
Missing spinal seeding

Failing to perform complete spine MRI in high-grade tumors can miss M1c disease. Always include spine imaging in the staging protocol for medulloepithelioma and other embryonal tumors.

3
Confusing reactive with neoplastic enhancement

Post-surgical changes can mimic tumor spread. Compare with pre-operative imaging and look for nodular rather than smooth enhancement patterns to identify true tumor spread.

4
Inadequate CSF assessment

Relying only on imaging for CSF involvement without cytological confirmation can lead to understaging. M1a requires cytological confirmation of tumor cells in CSF.

5
Ignoring molecular markers

Focusing only on imaging without considering molecular classification misses crucial prognostic information. IDH status, 1p/19q codeletion, and MGMT methylation are essential for glioma management.

6
Overlooking systemic metastases

While rare, some CNS tumors can metastasize systemically. Don't forget to evaluate for M1d disease in appropriate clinical contexts, especially with embryonal tumors.

7
Misinterpreting imaging artifacts

Motion artifacts, flow artifacts, or incomplete fat suppression can mimic pathological enhancement. Always correlate with clinical findings and consider repeat imaging if uncertain.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Tumor Type and Location

Identify the primary tumor type and anatomical location. Determine if TNM staging is applicable (primarily medulloepithelioma) or if WHO grading is more appropriate.

2
Assess Primary Tumor Characteristics

Evaluate tumor size, enhancement pattern, necrosis, hemorrhage, and local extension. Note involvement of eloquent areas and relationship to critical structures.

3
Evaluate Ventricular System

Systematically examine all ventricles for ependymal enhancement, nodular deposits, or hydrocephalus. Use thin-section post-contrast T1-weighted images.

4
Assess Meningeal Involvement

Look for leptomeningeal enhancement, particularly nodular enhancement which is more concerning than smooth enhancement. Include basal cisterns and cortical sulci.

5
Complete Spine Evaluation

Perform complete spine MRI for high-grade tumors. Look for subarachnoid nodular enhancement or masses. This is crucial for M1c staging in medulloepithelioma.

6
Consider Systemic Evaluation

For appropriate tumor types, consider chest CT and bone marrow evaluation for systemic metastases (M1d). This is particularly relevant for embryonal tumors.

7
Correlate with CSF Analysis

If available, correlate imaging findings with CSF cytology results. Positive CSF cytology constitutes M1a disease and affects staging and treatment planning.

8
Integrate Molecular Information

Incorporate available molecular markers (IDH, 1p/19q, MGMT) into the final assessment. These are often more important than traditional staging for treatment planning.

Clinical Pearls for FRCR Preparation

Key Examination Points

  • Know that TNM staging is NOT routinely used for most CNS tumors
  • Understand that WHO grading is the primary classification system
  • Remember that M staging for medulloepithelioma follows Chang criteria
  • Be able to identify CSF seeding patterns on imaging
  • Understand the importance of molecular markers in glioma classification
  • Know when to recommend complete spine imaging
  • Recognize the limitations of size-based staging in CNS tumors

T Stage Criteria by Subsite

Supraglottic Larynx

T Stage Criteria
T1 Tumor limited to one subsite of supraglottis with normal vocal cord mobility
T2 Tumor invades mucosa of more than one adjacent subsite of supraglottis or glottis or region outside the supraglottis without fixation of the larynx
T3 Tumor limited to larynx with vocal cord fixation and/or invades any of: postcricoid area, pre-epiglottic space, paraglottic space, inner cortex of thyroid cartilage
T4a Tumor invades through thyroid cartilage and/or invades tissues beyond the larynx
T4b Tumor invades prevertebral space, encases carotid artery, or invades mediastinal structures

Glottic Larynx

T Stage Criteria
T1 T1a: Tumor limited to one vocal cord
T1b: Tumor involves both vocal cords
T2 Tumor extends to supraglottis and/or subglottis, and/or with impaired vocal cord mobility
T3 Tumor limited to larynx with vocal cord fixation and/or invasion of paraglottic space and/or inner cortex of thyroid cartilage
T4a Tumor invades through thyroid cartilage and/or invades tissues beyond the larynx
T4b Tumor invades prevertebral space, encases carotid artery, or invades mediastinal structures

Subglottic Larynx

T Stage Criteria
T1 Tumor limited to subglottis
T2 Tumor extends to vocal cord(s) with normal or impaired mobility
T3 Tumor limited to larynx with vocal cord fixation and/or invasion of paraglottic space and/or inner cortex of thyroid or cricoid cartilage
T4a Tumor invades through cricoid or thyroid cartilage and/or invades tissues beyond the larynx
T4b Tumor invades prevertebral space, encases carotid artery, or invades mediastinal structures

N Stage Classification

N Stage Clinical Criteria
N0 No regional lymph node metastasis
N1 Metastasis in a single ipsilateral lymph node, ≤3 cm and ENE(-)
N2a Metastasis in a single ipsilateral lymph node, >3 cm but ≤6 cm and ENE(-)
N2b Metastasis in multiple ipsilateral lymph nodes, none >6 cm and ENE(-)
N2c Metastasis in bilateral or contralateral lymph nodes, none >6 cm and ENE(-)
N3a Metastasis in a lymph node >6 cm and ENE(-)
N3b Metastasis in any node(s) with clinically overt ENE(+)

Key Imaging Tips

1. Cartilage Invasion Assessment

Look for sclerosis, erosion, or lysis of laryngeal cartilages

MRI is superior to CT for detecting cartilage invasion. T2-weighted images show high signal in invaded cartilage. Contrast enhancement indicates invasion.

2. Paraglottic Space Evaluation

Assess fat planes lateral to the ventricles and vocal cords

Axial and coronal images are essential. Loss of the paraglottic fat indicates invasion. This finding upgrades to at least T3.

3. Pre-epiglottic Space Assessment

Evaluate the fat-filled space anterior to the epiglottis

Best seen on sagittal images. Invasion appears as replacement of fat signal. Important for supraglottic tumors - indicates T3 disease.

4. Vocal Cord Mobility

Dynamic assessment during phonation is crucial

Use "E" phonation during CT or real-time MRI. Fixed cord indicates T3 disease. Correlate with laryngoscopy findings when available.

5. Submucosal Spread Detection

Look for asymmetric soft tissue thickening

Contrast-enhanced sequences improve detection. Submucosal spread often underestimated on endoscopy. Critical for accurate T staging.

6. Nodal Assessment Protocol

Measure short axis diameter, assess for ENE

Nodes >1cm in short axis are suspicious. Look for irregular margins, fat stranding for ENE. Central necrosis suggests malignancy regardless of size.

7. Carotid Encasement Evaluation

Assess degree of circumferential contact

>270° encasement indicates unresectability (T4b). Loss of fat plane alone is not sufficient. Use multiplanar reconstructions.

8. Post-treatment Changes

Distinguish recurrence from treatment effects

Baseline scan 3 months post-treatment is essential. PET-CT helpful after 12 weeks. Look for progressive enhancement and mass effect.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Overcalling Cartilage Invasion

Reactive sclerosis can mimic invasion. True invasion shows erosion, lysis, or extralaryngeal tumor extension through cartilage. Isolated sclerosis without other features is often reactive.

2
Missing Subglottic Extension

Always assess 2cm below the vocal cords. Subglottic extension changes surgical approach. Coronal reformats are essential for accurate assessment.

3
Confusing Arytenoid Sclerosis

Arytenoid sclerosis alone doesn't indicate invasion. Look for associated soft tissue mass, erosion, or abnormal enhancement. Weight-bearing sclerosis is physiologic.

4
Underestimating Transglottic Tumors

Tumors crossing the ventricle are at least T2. Don't focus only on the dominant component. Assess all three laryngeal subsites systematically.

5
Missing ENE on Imaging

Subtle ENE can be missed. Look for irregular nodal margins, fat stranding, and adjacent structure invasion. Clinical ENE (N3b) requires obvious signs.

6
Incorrect Vocal Cord Assessment

Impaired mobility ≠ fixation. Fixed cord requires complete immobility (T3). Partial movement indicates T2. Correlate with clinical findings.

7
Overlooking Postcricoid Involvement

Carefully assess the posterior larynx. Postcricoid involvement indicates T3 disease and affects surgical planning. Use multiple planes.

Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor Location

Determine the epicenter and primary subsite (supraglottic, glottic, or subglottic). Note if transglottic. This guides subsequent T staging criteria.

2
Assess Tumor Extent

Measure maximum dimension. Evaluate spread to adjacent subsites. Check for crossing of ventricle (transglottic = at least T2).

3
Evaluate Deep Spaces

Systematically check paraglottic space (axial/coronal), pre-epiglottic space (sagittal), and postcricoid region. Invasion indicates T3.

4
Assess Cartilage Status

Evaluate thyroid, cricoid, and arytenoid cartilages. Look for erosion, lysis, or tumor extension through cartilage (T4a). Inner cortex invasion alone = T3.

5
Check Extralaryngeal Extension

Look for invasion of strap muscles, thyroid gland, esophagus, or trachea (T4a). Assess prevertebral space and carotid encasement (T4b).

6
Lymph Node Assessment

Count and measure all suspicious nodes. Note laterality. Assess for ENE (irregular margins, fat stranding). Check retropharyngeal nodes.

7
Screen for Distant Metastases

Review lung apices on neck CT. Consider chest CT for advanced disease. Check for suspicious bone lesions. Note any liver abnormalities.

8
Synthesize and Double-Check

Compile findings into TNM stage. Verify against criteria tables. Ensure consistency between imaging findings and stage assignment. Document key findings.

Key Imaging Tips

Size Measurement

Always measure the largest dimension in any plane

Use the longest measurement from axial, coronal, or sagittal planes. Include any lobulated or irregular extensions in the measurement.

Depth Assessment

Determine relationship to superficial fascia

Superficial tumors are above the superficial fascia. Deep tumors are below or involve the fascia. This affects prognosis but not T stage in AJCC 9th edition.

Vascular Involvement

Look for vessel encasement or displacement

Complete encasement (>270°) suggests invasion. Simple displacement without encasement is usually not invasion. Use contrast-enhanced sequences.

Bone Invasion

Assess cortical integrity and marrow signal

Look for cortical destruction, marrow replacement, or tumor crossing cortex. Mere contact or pressure erosion may not represent true invasion.

Compartmental Analysis

Identify anatomical compartment involvement

Note which fascial compartments are involved. Trans-compartmental spread may indicate more aggressive behavior and affects surgical planning.

Metastasis Screening

Systematic evaluation of common sites

Lungs are most common site (85%). Also check liver, bones, and other soft tissue sites. Use chest CT for lung metastases detection.

Lymph Node Assessment

Rare but important when present

Most sarcomas don't spread to lymph nodes except epithelioid, clear cell, angiosarcoma, and rhabdomyosarcoma. Size >1cm is suspicious.

Post-Treatment Changes

Distinguish recurrence from treatment effects

Post-radiation changes can mimic recurrence. Use DWI and contrast enhancement patterns. Compare with baseline post-treatment imaging.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Ignoring Histologic Grade

Grade is crucial for staging. A T4 Grade 1 tumor is only Stage IB, not Stage III. Always confirm the FNCLCC grade before finalizing stage.

2
Incorrect Size Measurement

Measuring only in one plane or excluding irregular extensions. Always use the largest dimension in any imaging plane including all tumor components.

3
Overdiagnosing Vascular Invasion

Simple vessel displacement is not invasion. Look for true encasement (>270°), vessel wall irregularity, or luminal compromise before calling invasion.

4
Missing Retroperitoneal Differences

Retroperitoneal sarcomas have different staging criteria. Size cutoffs and invasion patterns differ from extremity tumors.

5
Assuming All Sarcomas Avoid Nodes

While most sarcomas rarely metastasize to lymph nodes, some subtypes (epithelioid, clear cell, angiosarcoma) do. Don't ignore enlarged nodes.

6
Inadequate Metastasis Survey

Chest X-ray is insufficient for lung metastases. Always use chest CT. Don't forget to evaluate bones and liver, especially in high-grade tumors.

7
Confusing Depth with T Stage

In AJCC 9th edition, superficial vs deep location doesn't affect T stage for extremity sarcomas - only size matters for T staging.

Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Tumor Location

Identify the primary anatomical site (extremity, trunk, retroperitoneum, etc.) as this determines which staging criteria to apply.

2
Measure Maximum Dimension

Measure the largest tumor dimension in any imaging plane. Include all components and irregular extensions in the measurement.

3
Assess Local Invasion

Evaluate for bone invasion, major vessel encasement, and adjacent organ involvement. Look for fascial plane disruption.

4
Evaluate Regional Nodes

Assess regional lymph node stations. Measure suspicious nodes and note morphology, though nodal metastases are rare in most sarcomas.

5
Screen for Distant Metastases

Systematically evaluate lungs (most common), liver, bones, and other soft tissue sites. Use appropriate imaging sequences.

6
Determine Compartmental Involvement

Identify which anatomical compartments are involved and whether there is trans-compartmental spread affecting surgical planning.

7
Correlate with Histologic Grade

Confirm the FNCLCC grade from pathology as this is essential for accurate stage grouping. Grade 1 tumors cannot be Stage III.

8
Assign Final TNM Stage

Combine T, N, M, and Grade to determine overall stage group. Consider location-specific variations in staging criteria.

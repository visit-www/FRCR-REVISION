Key Imaging Assessment Tips

MRI T2-Weighted Imaging

Primary modality for local staging. Tumor appears hyperintense compared to normal cervical stroma.

Use sagittal and axial planes. Intravaginal gel improves vaginal assessment.

Parametrial Invasion Assessment

Look for disruption of the hypointense cervical stromal ring on T2WI.

Spiculated or irregular tumor margins extending into parametrium indicate invasion.

Pelvic Sidewall Extension

Measure distance from tumor edge to pelvic sidewall muscles.

≤3mm distance suggests sidewall involvement. Look for loss of fat plane.

Vaginal Extension Assessment

Identify upper vs lower 1/3 involvement - critical for Stage II vs III distinction.

Lower 1/3 extends from introitus to level of bladder base.

Lymph Node Evaluation

Size criteria: >1cm short axis for pelvic nodes, >8mm for para-aortic nodes.

PET/CT helpful for metabolically active nodes of any size.

Hydronephrosis Detection

Assess for ureteral obstruction - automatically upgrades to Stage IIIB.

Look for dilated collecting system and delayed nephrogram on contrast studies.

Bladder/Rectal Invasion

Requires mucosal involvement, not just wall thickening or contact.

Cystoscopy/proctoscopy with biopsy needed for confirmation.

DWI and ADC Mapping

Tumor shows restricted diffusion (high DWI signal, low ADC values).

Useful for detecting small lesions and assessing treatment response.

Common Staging Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Bladder Wall Thickening with Mucosal Invasion

Stage IVA requires proven mucosal invasion by cystoscopy/biopsy. Wall thickening alone is insufficient and may be inflammatory.

2
Misidentifying Vaginal Thirds

Lower 1/3 extends from introitus to bladder base level. Upper 2/3 involvement = Stage IIA, lower 1/3 = Stage IIIA.

3
Overlooking Hydronephrosis

Any degree of hydronephrosis or non-functioning kidney automatically upgrades to Stage IIIB, regardless of other findings.

4
Inadequate Lymph Node Assessment

Must evaluate entire pelvic and para-aortic chain. Single positive node changes staging significantly (Stage III).

5
Ignoring Size Cutoffs for Stage IB

Stage IB subdivisions are critical: IB1 (<2cm), IB2 (2-4cm), IB3 (≥4cm). Treatment approaches differ significantly.

6
Parametrial vs Paracervical Tissue

True parametrial invasion requires extension beyond the cervical stroma into the cardinal/uterosacral ligaments.

7
Missing Microscopic Disease Criteria

Stage IA requires stromal invasion ≤5mm depth. If clinically visible or >5mm invasion, it's at least Stage IB1.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Primary Site

Verify tumor originates from cervix, not vagina or uterine corpus. Check transformation zone involvement.

2
Measure Maximum Tumor Dimension

Measure in all three planes. Record greatest dimension for Stage IB subdivision (2cm and 4cm cutoffs).

3
Assess Parametrial Extension

Look for disruption of hypointense stromal ring. Spiculated margins or loss of fat planes indicate invasion.

4
Evaluate Vaginal Extension

Determine if upper 2/3 (Stage IIA) or lower 1/3 (Stage IIIA). Use bladder base as anatomical landmark.

5
Check for Pelvic Sidewall Extension

Measure distance to pelvic muscles. ≤3mm suggests sidewall involvement (Stage IIIB).

6
Assess Urinary Tract

Look for hydronephrosis, ureteral obstruction, or bladder invasion. Any hydronephrosis = Stage IIIB minimum.

7
Evaluate Lymph Nodes

Assess pelvic and para-aortic chains. Size criteria and PET activity. Positive nodes = Stage IIIC.

8
Screen for Distant Disease

Check for extra-pelvic metastases, peritoneal disease, distant organ involvement (Stage IVB).

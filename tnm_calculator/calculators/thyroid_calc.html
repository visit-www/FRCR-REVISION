Key Imaging Tips

Strap Muscle Invasion (T3b)

Look for loss of fat plane between thyroid and strap muscles on axial images.

Contrast enhancement of strap muscles may indicate invasion. Compare with contralateral side.

Tracheal Invasion (T4a)

Assess for loss of normal air-soft tissue interface and tracheal wall thickening.

Look for intraluminal extension or cartilage destruction on CT. MRI better shows soft tissue invasion.

Esophageal Invasion (T4a)

Loss of fat plane between thyroid and esophagus, especially on lateral views.

Barium swallow may show mucosal irregularity if invasion is transmural.

Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve (T4a)

Indirect signs: vocal cord paralysis on dynamic imaging or clinical exam.

Direct visualization of nerve invasion is difficult. Look for mass in tracheoesophageal groove.

Carotid Encasement (T4b)

Tumor surrounds >180° of carotid artery circumference.

Measure on axial images. Look for loss of normal fat plane and vessel deformity.

Central vs Lateral Neck Nodes

Level VI (central) = N1a. Levels I-V, VII (lateral/retropharyngeal) = N1b.

Central compartment bounded by hyoid superiorly, suprasternal notch inferiorly, carotid sheaths laterally.

Suspicious Nodal Features

Round shape, loss of fatty hilum, microcalcifications, cystic change.

Size criteria less reliable in thyroid cancer. Morphology more important than size.

Distant Metastases

Most common sites: lungs (miliary pattern), bones (lytic), liver.

Pulmonary metastases may be too small to detect on initial CT. Consider high-resolution technique.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing abutment with invasion

Simple contact with adjacent structures doesn't equal invasion. Look for loss of fat planes, irregular interfaces, and enhancement patterns to confirm true invasion.

2
Forgetting age-dependent staging

Patients <55 years with differentiated thyroid cancer can only be Stage I or II, regardless of T or N stage. Age ≥55 uses traditional staging.

3
Misclassifying nodal levels

Level VI (central compartment) = N1a. All other cervical levels = N1b. Don't use size criteria for thyroid nodal staging.

4
Overlooking minimal extrathyroidal extension

Even microscopic extension beyond thyroid capsule affects staging. Look carefully at thyroid margins, especially posteriorly.

5
Missing anaplastic transformation

All anaplastic thyroid cancers are T4 minimum. Look for rapid growth, necrosis, and aggressive features that suggest dedifferentiation.

6
Inadequate assessment of tracheal invasion

Tracheal invasion (T4a) requires more than just contact. Look for cartilage destruction, intraluminal extension, or wall thickening with enhancement.

7
Confusing reactive and metastatic nodes

In thyroid cancer, morphology trumps size. Small nodes with suspicious features (round, no hilum, calcifications) may be metastatic.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Patient Demographics

Note patient age (critical for staging) and review clinical history for histologic type and prior treatments.

2
Identify Primary Tumor

Locate and measure the dominant thyroid lesion. Note which lobe, relationship to isthmus, and multifocality.

3
Assess Thyroid Capsule

Examine thyroid margins systematically. Look for capsular breach, especially posteriorly where capsule is thinnest.

4
Evaluate Strap Muscle Invasion

Check fat planes between thyroid and strap muscles. Loss of plane + muscle enhancement suggests T3b disease.

5
Check Critical Structures (T4a)

Systematically assess trachea, esophagus, larynx, and subcutaneous tissues. Look for loss of normal interfaces.

6
Assess Vascular Involvement (T4b)

Examine carotid arteries for encasement (>180°). Check for mediastinal vessel involvement and prevertebral fascia extension.

7
Evaluate Lymph Nodes

Systematically review central compartment (Level VI) then lateral neck (Levels I-V). Note size, morphology, and enhancement.

8
Screen for Distant Disease

Check lungs for miliary pattern, bones for lytic lesions, and liver for metastases. Review any additional imaging.

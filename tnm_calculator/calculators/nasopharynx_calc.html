T Stage Quick Reference

T Stage Key Features Critical Imaging Points
T1 Nasopharynx ± oropharynx/nasal cavity NO parapharyngeal involvement
T2 Parapharyngeal space OR pterygoid/prevertebral muscles Fat plane obliteration, muscle enhancement
T3 Bony infiltration (skull base, sinuses, vertebrae) Erosion OR permeation (both count)
T4 Intracranial, cranial nerves, orbit, extensive soft tissue Cavernous sinus, nerve enhancement, orbital involvement

N Stage Quick Reference

N Stage Distribution Size Limit Location ENE
N1 Unilateral cervical OR retropharyngeal (uni/bilateral) ≤6 cm Above cricoid No advanced ENE
N2 Bilateral cervical ≤6 cm Above cricoid No advanced ENE
N3 Any distribution >6 cm OR any size Below cricoid OR any Advanced ENE

Key Imaging Tips

Parapharyngeal Space Assessment

Look for obliteration of the normal fat planes around the internal carotid artery and styloid process.

T2 criterion: Any parapharyngeal involvement = T2 minimum

Skull Base Evaluation

Assess both erosive changes and signal abnormality/enhancement within bone marrow.

T3 criterion: Erosion OR permeation both qualify

Cranial Nerve Enhancement

Look for abnormal enhancement of trigeminal (V) and abducens (VI) nerves - most commonly affected.

T4 criterion: Unequivocal radiological involvement required

Retropharyngeal Nodes

Often the first site of nodal spread. Size >5mm is suspicious, >8mm is definitely pathological.

N1 criterion: Unilateral or bilateral retropharyngeal = N1

Cavernous Sinus Involvement

Look for loss of normal CSF signal, abnormal enhancement, or mass effect on cavernous sinus contents.

T4 criterion: Intracranial extension includes cavernous sinus

Prevertebral Muscle Assessment

Evaluate longus capitis and longus colli muscles for abnormal signal or enhancement.

T2 criterion: Prevertebral muscle involvement = T2

Pterygoid Muscle Evaluation

Assess both medial and lateral pterygoid muscles for signal abnormality and enhancement.

T2 criterion: Either pterygoid muscle involvement = T2

Advanced ENE Recognition

Look for irregular nodal borders, adjacent muscle invasion, skin involvement, or neurovascular bundle encasement.

N3 criterion: Advanced ENE automatically = N3

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Missing Subtle Skull Base Permeation

Don't rely only on gross erosion. Signal abnormality within bone marrow on T1 and enhancement on post-contrast images also indicate T3 disease, even without obvious cortical destruction.

2
Overlooking Retropharyngeal Nodes

These are often the first and sometimes only nodal involvement. Bilateral retropharyngeal nodes still = N1, not N2. Size >5mm should raise suspicion.

3
Confusing Parapharyngeal vs. Masticator Space

Parapharyngeal involvement = T2. Extension beyond the anterolateral surface of lateral pterygoid = T4. The distinction is critical for staging.

4
Underestimating Prevertebral Muscle Involvement

Any involvement of longus capitis or longus colli muscles = T2. This is often subtle and requires careful evaluation of muscle signal and enhancement.

5
Missing Bilateral Cervical Node Disease

Bilateral cervical nodes = N2, even if retropharyngeal nodes are also present. Don't forget to assess both sides of the neck systematically.

6
Inadequate Assessment of Cranial Nerve Involvement

Cranial nerve enhancement must be "unequivocal" for T4. Subtle enhancement may be reactive. Look for nerve thickening, abnormal signal, and clinical correlation.

7
Forgetting the Cricoid Landmark

Nodes below the caudal border of cricoid cartilage = N3, regardless of size. This anatomical landmark is crucial for N staging.

8
Misinterpreting T0 Disease

T0 specifically refers to EBV-positive cervical nodes without identifiable primary tumor. This is different from occult primary tumors in other head and neck sites.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor Location

Confirm nasopharyngeal location and assess for extension to oropharynx or nasal cavity. Document if confined to nasopharynx only.

2
Evaluate Parapharyngeal Space

Look for obliteration of fat planes around internal carotid artery. Any parapharyngeal involvement = minimum T2 disease.

3
Assess Muscle Involvement

Systematically evaluate medial pterygoid, lateral pterygoid, and prevertebral muscles (longus capitis/colli) for signal abnormality and enhancement.

4
Examine Skull Base and Bone

Assess for erosion or signal abnormality in skull base, pterygoid plates, paranasal sinuses, and cervical vertebrae. Both erosion and permeation count as T3.

5
Check for T4 Features

Look for intracranial extension, cavernous sinus involvement, cranial nerve enhancement, orbital involvement, and extensive soft tissue infiltration beyond lateral pterygoid.

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Start with retropharyngeal nodes, then bilateral cervical chains. Measure largest node, count distribution, and assess for ENE.

7
Apply Cricoid Landmark

Identify caudal border of cricoid cartilage. Any nodes below this level = N3, regardless of other factors.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Assess for liver, lung, bone, and other distant metastases. Count total number of lesions for M1a (≤3) vs M1b (>3) classification.

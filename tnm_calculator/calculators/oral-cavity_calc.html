Key Imaging Tips

Depth of Invasion Measurement

DOI is measured from the basement membrane of the closest normal mucosa, NOT from the tumor surface.

Use reconstructed sagittal/coronal images for accurate DOI measurement perpendicular to mucosal surface.

Cortical Bone Assessment

Look for complete loss of cortical bone signal on all sequences, not just erosion or thinning.

Bone windows on CT are essential - MRI can miss early cortical involvement.

Masticator Space Invasion

Assess pterygoid muscles, masseter, and temporalis for tumor infiltration.

T2 hyperintensity and enhancement within muscle indicates invasion, not just contact.

ENE Detection

Look for irregular node margins, surrounding fat stranding, and loss of capsular definition.

Clinical ENE requires unambiguous evidence - matted nodes, skin invasion, or muscle infiltration.

Maxillary Sinus Involvement

Assess for mucosal thickening, fluid levels, or direct tumor extension into sinus cavity.

Differentiate inflammatory changes from tumor involvement using enhancement patterns.

Carotid Encasement

Tumor surrounding >270° of vessel circumference indicates encasement.

Use axial images at multiple levels - partial encasement may progress to complete.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Tumor Thickness with DOI

DOI is measured from normal mucosal basement membrane, not tumor surface. Exophytic tumors can have large thickness but minimal DOI.

2
Superficial Bone Erosion vs Through Cortical Bone

Superficial erosion of tooth socket by gingival primary is NOT T4a. Must see complete cortical breakthrough.

3
Overcalling ENE on Imaging

Clinical ENE requires unambiguous evidence. Subtle fat stranding or irregular margins alone are insufficient for cN3b.

4
Missing Size + DOI Interaction

Both size AND DOI matter for T1-T3. A 1.5cm tumor with 8mm DOI is T2, not T1, despite small size.

5
Inflammatory vs Tumor Enhancement

Post-biopsy changes can mimic tumor extension. Correlate with clinical timeline and biopsy sites.

6
Muscle Contact vs Invasion

Tumor contact with muscle is not invasion. Look for T2 signal change and enhancement within muscle fibers.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Site

Confirm anatomical subsite within oral cavity. Check if tumor crosses anatomical boundaries.

2
Measure Tumor Size

Record maximum dimension in any plane. Use multiplanar reconstructions for accuracy.

3
Assess Depth of Invasion

Measure from normal mucosal basement membrane perpendicular to surface. Use sagittal/coronal planes.

4
Check for T4b Features

Systematically assess masticator space, pterygoid plates, skull base, and carotid encasement.

5
Evaluate T4a Features

Look for cortical bone breakthrough, maxillary sinus involvement, and skin invasion.

6
Survey Lymph Nodes

Measure largest node, count bilateral nodes, assess for ENE signs (irregular margins, fat stranding).

7
Screen for Distant Disease

Check lung bases, liver, bone for metastases. Review any PET findings if available.

8
Integrate Findings

Combine T, N, M stages using AJCC criteria. Double-check size/DOI interactions for T1-T3.

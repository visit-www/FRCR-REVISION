Key Imaging Tips

CT vs MRI for Bone Assessment

CT is superior for identifying bone erosion of thin walls and septa of paranasal sinuses.

Use thin-section CT (≤1mm) for optimal bone detail assessment

MRI for Tumor Mapping

T2-weighted MRI helps distinguish tumor from obstructed secretions.

Tumor typically shows intermediate T2 signal vs high T2 of retained secretions

Ohngren's Line Significance

Divides maxillary sinus into infrastructure (good prognosis) and suprastructure (poor prognosis).

Line from medial canthus to angle of mandible - suprastructure tumors invade critical structures early

Cribriform Plate Assessment

Critical for T3/T4a staging - look for asymmetry and enhancement.

Compare both sides; subtle asymmetry may indicate early invasion

Orbital Invasion Patterns

Distinguish floor/medial wall (T3) from anterior contents (T4a) from apex (T4b).

Periorbital fat stranding suggests early invasion; muscle involvement confirms T4a

Pterygoid Plate Distinction

Pterygoid fossa invasion = T3; pterygoid plate invasion = T4a.

Fossa is soft tissue space; plates are bony structures - different T stages

Dural Enhancement Patterns

Distinguish reactive enhancement from true dural invasion (T4b).

Nodular enhancement and dural thickening >5mm suggest invasion

ENE Assessment on Imaging

Look for irregular nodal borders, fat stranding, and adjacent structure invasion.

Imaging has limited accuracy for ENE - clinical correlation essential

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Pterygoid Fossa vs Plates

Pterygoid fossa invasion = T3, but pterygoid plate invasion = T4a. The fossa is the soft tissue space between the plates, while the plates themselves are bony structures requiring higher staging.

2
Mistaking Retained Secretions for Tumor

Obstructed sinuses can mimic tumor extension. Use T2-weighted MRI to distinguish high T2 secretions from intermediate T2 tumor tissue. Always correlate with enhancement patterns.

3
Overlooking Minimal Dural Enhancement

Subtle dural enhancement may be reactive rather than invasive. Look for nodular enhancement, dural thickening >5mm, and associated brain edema to confirm T4b staging.

4
Incorrect Orbital Invasion Assessment

Distinguish between periorbital fat stranding (possible early invasion) and definite muscle/nerve involvement (confirmed T4a). Fat stranding alone may not warrant T4a staging.

5
Missing Contralateral Node Assessment

Always assess bilateral neck nodes. Even small contralateral nodes can upstage to N2c. Don't focus only on the ipsilateral side, especially in midline or extensive tumors.

6
Overestimating ENE on Imaging

Imaging has limited accuracy for ENE detection. Irregular nodal borders and fat stranding are suggestive but not definitive. Clinical examination findings are crucial for ENE assessment.

7
Forgetting Subsite-Specific Criteria

Maxillary sinus and nasal cavity/ethmoid have different T-staging criteria. Ensure you're applying the correct criteria based on the primary tumor location - they are not interchangeable.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Site

Determine if tumor originates from maxillary sinus, nasal cavity, or ethmoid sinus. This determines which T-staging criteria to apply.

2
Assess for T4b Features First

Look for brain, dura, orbital apex, middle cranial fossa, cranial nerve (non-V2), nasopharynx, or clivus invasion. Any of these = automatic T4b.

3
Check T4a Criteria

If no T4b features, assess for anterior orbital contents, skin, pterygoid plates, infratemporal fossa, cribriform plate, or sphenoid/frontal sinus invasion.

4
Evaluate T3 Features

Look for specific T3 criteria based on subsite: posterior maxillary wall, orbit floor/medial wall, pterygoid fossa, subcutaneous tissues, or ethmoid involvement.

5
Assess Bone Involvement

Use CT to evaluate bone erosion patterns. Different bone involvement patterns correspond to different T stages depending on the primary site.

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Measure largest node, count nodes by location (ipsilateral, contralateral, bilateral), and assess for ENE using size, border irregularity, and fat stranding.

7
Search for Distant Metastases

Evaluate for lung, liver, bone, and other distant metastases. Consider PET-CT for advanced local disease or suspicious clinical findings.

8
Correlate with Clinical Findings

Review clinical examination, endoscopy findings, and symptoms. Clinical correlation is essential, especially for ENE assessment and symptom correlation.

Larynx Cancer TNM Staging

AJCC 9th Edition (2024)
Interactive staging calculator for laryngeal carcinoma covering all subsites: glottis, supraglottis, and subglottis. Emphasizes cartilage invasion criteria and voice preservation considerations for optimal treatment planning.
Primary Tumor (T) Assessment

Clinical Approach: Work through each category systematically, starting with the most advanced. Consider imaging findings (CT/MRI) and endoscopic assessment.

T4b - Very Advanced Local Disease ▼

Tumor invades prevertebral fascia OR encases carotid artery OR involves mediastinal structures

🎯 Clinical Pearl: "PACE" mnemonic - Prevertebral fascia, Artery encasement (carotid), Central compartment involvement, Extensive mediastinal involvement
  • Prevertebral fascia invasion (posterior boundary)
  • Carotid artery encasement (>270° circumference)
  • Mediastinal structure involvement (esophagus, great vessels)
  • Extensive soft tissue invasion of neck
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Don't confuse carotid contact/abutment with true encasement. Encasement requires >270° circumferential involvement on axial imaging.

Voice Preservation:

T4b tumors are generally not candidates for voice-preserving surgery. Total laryngectomy is typically required.

T4a - Advanced Local Disease ▼

Tumor invades through outer cortex of thyroid cartilage OR invades tissues beyond larynx

🔍 Imaging Key: Look for thyroid cartilage cortical breakthrough on CT. MRI better for soft tissue extension assessment.
  • Through outer cortex of thyroid cartilage
  • Trachea invasion
  • Soft tissues of neck
  • Deep extrinsic muscle of tongue
  • Strap muscles
  • Thyroid gland
  • Esophagus

Subsite Considerations:

Supraglottis: Extension to tongue base, vallecula, or pyriform sinus apex
Glottis: Extension to supraglottis/subglottis with fixation
Subglottis: Extension to cricoid cartilage or trachea

⚠️ Clinical Note: Some T4a lesions may still be candidates for voice-preserving surgery depending on extent and location.
T3 - Moderately Advanced Local Disease ▼

Vocal cord fixation OR paraglottic space invasion OR inner cortex of thyroid cartilage

🎵 Voice Assessment: Vocal cord fixation is key - assess on direct laryngoscopy with patient phonation. Fixation indicates cricoarytenoid joint or posterior cricoarytenoid muscle involvement.

Subsite-Specific T3 Criteria:

Supraglottis:

  • Vocal cord fixation
  • Invasion of postcricoid area
  • Pre-epiglottic space
  • Paraglottic space
  • Minor thyroid cartilage erosion (inner cortex)

Glottis:

  • Vocal cord fixation
  • Paraglottic space invasion
  • Minor thyroid cartilage erosion (inner cortex only)

Subglottis:

  • Vocal cord fixation
  • Paraglottic space invasion
  • Minor thyroid cartilage erosion (inner cortex)
⚠️ Cartilage Invasion Key: T3 = inner cortex only. T4a = through outer cortex (complete breakthrough). This distinction is crucial for treatment planning.
T2 - Intermediate Local Disease ▼

Tumor extends to adjacent subsites within larynx with normal vocal cord mobility

🔑 Key Point: Normal vocal cord mobility is essential for T2. Any fixation upgrades to T3.

Subsite-Specific T2 Criteria:

Supraglottis:

  • Mucosa of more than one adjacent subsite of supraglottis
  • Glottis extension with normal vocal cord mobility
  • Region outside supraglottis (tongue base, vallecula, medial wall pyriform sinus)

Glottis:

  • Extends to supraglottis and/or subglottis
  • Normal or impaired vocal cord mobility (but NOT fixed)

Subglottis:

  • Extends to vocal cord(s) with normal or impaired mobility
⚠️ Mobility Assessment: Distinguish between impaired mobility (still T2) and true fixation (T3). Impaired = reduced but present movement.
T1 - Limited Local Disease ▼

Tumor limited to subsite of origin with normal vocal cord mobility

Subsite-Specific T1 Criteria:

Supraglottis (T1):

  • Limited to one subsite with normal vocal cord mobility

Glottis:

  • T1a: Limited to one vocal cord
  • T1b: Involves both vocal cords

Subglottis (T1):

  • Limited to subglottis
🎯 Treatment Pearl: T1 glottic cancers are excellent candidates for voice-preserving treatment (radiation therapy or endoscopic resection).
Tis - Carcinoma in Situ ▼

Carcinoma in situ

🔬 Histology: Requires pathologic confirmation. Full-thickness epithelial dysplasia without basement membrane invasion.
⚠️ Clinical Note: Tis requires complete pathologic assessment. Cannot be determined by imaging alone.
Regional Lymph Nodes (N) Assessment
📏 Size Measurement: Measure largest dimension in short axis on cross-sectional imaging. Pathologic N staging uses largest dimension.

Clinical N Staging (cN):

  • cN0: No regional lymph node metastasis
  • cN1: Single ipsilateral lymph node ≤3 cm
  • cN2a: Single ipsilateral lymph node >3 cm but ≤6 cm
  • cN2b: Multiple ipsilateral lymph nodes, none >6 cm
  • cN2c: Bilateral or contralateral lymph nodes, none >6 cm
  • cN3a: Lymph node >6 cm
  • cN3b: Clinical extranodal extension
⚠️ Larynx-Specific: Laryngeal cancers have lower rates of nodal metastasis compared to other head and neck sites. Glottic cancers have the lowest nodal involvement rate.
Distant Metastasis (M) Assessment
  • M0: No distant metastasis
  • M1: Distant metastasis present
🔍 Common Sites: Lung, bone, liver. Chest CT is standard staging imaging. Consider PET/CT for advanced disease.

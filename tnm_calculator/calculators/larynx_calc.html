Key Imaging Tips

Vocal Cord Assessment

Evaluate cord mobility on dynamic imaging if available

Fixed = complete loss of movement. Impaired = reduced but present movement. This distinction is critical for T2 vs T3.

Paraglottic Space

Look for fat stranding or mass effect in paraglottic fat

Paraglottic space invasion automatically makes it T3. Compare with normal side for subtle changes.

Cartilage Invasion Assessment

Distinguish inner cortex sclerosis from through-and-through invasion

Inner cortex erosion only = T3. Complete breakthrough of outer cortex = T4a. Use bone windows on CT.

Pre-epiglottic Space

Assess fat obliteration in pre-epiglottic space

Important for supraglottic tumors. Invasion makes it T3. Look for loss of normal fat signal/density.

Subglottic Extension

Measure extension >10mm below true vocal cords

>10mm below glottis suggests subglottic primary. Important for staging and treatment planning.

Carotid Encasement

Measure degree of carotid artery circumferential involvement

>270° encasement = T4b (unresectable). Use axial images to measure circumferential involvement accurately.

Nodal ENE Assessment

Look for irregular nodal borders and surrounding fat stranding

ENE upgrades any N stage to N3b. Look for spiculated margins, surrounding inflammatory changes.

Thyroid Cartilage Windows

Use appropriate CT windows for cartilage assessment

Bone windows (WW: 2000, WL: 400) best for detecting cartilage invasion. Soft tissue windows may miss subtle changes.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Impaired vs Fixed Vocal Cords

Impaired mobility = T2, Fixed = T3. This is a critical distinction that changes staging significantly. Always assess dynamic function when possible.

2
Missing T1a vs T1b for Glottic Tumors

T1a involves one vocal cord, T1b involves both. This subdivision is important for treatment planning and prognosis.

3
Overcalling Cartilage Invasion

Cartilage sclerosis alone doesn't equal invasion. Need actual erosion/breakthrough. Inner cortex only = T3, through outer cortex = T4a.

4
Missing Paraglottic Space Invasion

Subtle fat stranding can be easily missed. Always compare with the contralateral side and look for asymmetric fat obliteration.

5
Not Specifying Larynx Subsite

Different T staging criteria apply to glottic, supraglottic, and subglottic tumors. Always identify the primary subsite first.

6
Underestimating ENE Impact

Any extranodal extension automatically makes it N3b regardless of node size or number. Look carefully for irregular nodal margins.

7
Confusing Arytenoid Fixation

Arytenoid cartilage fixation can mimic vocal cord fixation. Assess true vocal cord movement specifically, not just arytenoid position.

8
Missing Subglottic Extension Measurement

Extension >10mm below the vocal cords suggests subglottic primary. Measure carefully on coronal images for accurate assessment.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Subsite

Determine if tumor is primarily glottic, supraglottic, or subglottic. This determines which T staging criteria to apply.

2
Assess Vocal Cord Mobility

Evaluate cord movement on dynamic imaging or clinical correlation. Distinguish normal, impaired, or fixed mobility.

3
Check for T4b Features First

Look for prevertebral fascia invasion, carotid encasement >270°, or mediastinal involvement. If present, automatically T4b.

4
Evaluate T4a Features

Assess for through-and-through cartilage invasion, tracheal invasion, soft tissue extension, or organ invasion.

5
Look for T3 Criteria

Check for vocal cord fixation, paraglottic space invasion, inner cortex cartilage erosion, or pre-epiglottic space involvement.

6
Apply Subsite-Specific T1/T2 Criteria

Use appropriate criteria based on primary subsite. Consider extent of disease and cord involvement patterns.

7
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Measure largest node, count bilateral nodes, assess for ENE. Remember ENE makes any node N3b.

8
Search for Distant Metastases

Evaluate lungs, liver, bones for metastatic disease. Consider PET/CT correlation if available.

Treatment Implications by Stage

Voice Preservation Considerations

Early stage disease (T1-T2) often amenable to voice-preserving treatments. Advanced disease may require total laryngectomy.

Stage Treatment Options Voice Preservation
T1-T2 Radiation therapy, laser resection, partial laryngectomy High likelihood
T3 Organ preservation protocols, chemoradiation Possible with protocols
T4a Total laryngectomy ± adjuvant therapy Usually not possible
T4b Palliative care, clinical trials Not applicable

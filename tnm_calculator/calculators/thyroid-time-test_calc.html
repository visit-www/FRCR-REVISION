Special Histology Considerations

Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma

ALL anaplastic thyroid carcinomas are considered T4

T4a: Intrathyroidal anaplastic carcinoma
T4b: Extrathyroidal anaplastic carcinoma

Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma

Uses same T, N, M criteria as differentiated thyroid cancer

Age does NOT affect staging for medullary carcinoma

Key Imaging Tips

Thyroid Capsule Assessment

Look for loss of echogenic thyroid capsule on ultrasound

Irregular, interrupted, or absent capsule suggests extrathyroidal extension. Correlate with CT/MRI for confirmation.

Strap Muscle Invasion

Assess for tumor extension into sternohyoid, sternothyroid muscles

Look for loss of fat plane between thyroid and strap muscles. Muscle enhancement or thickening suggests invasion.

Tracheal Invasion Assessment

Evaluate tracheal cartilage and mucosa on CT/MRI

>180° circumferential contact suggests invasion. Look for cartilage destruction or mucosal irregularity.

Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve

Assess tracheoesophageal groove for tumor extension

RLN runs in tracheoesophageal groove. Tumor in this location may indicate nerve involvement.

Lymph Node Criteria

Use size, shape, and enhancement patterns for assessment

Central nodes >6mm, lateral nodes >8mm are suspicious. Look for round shape, cystic change, calcifications.

Distant Metastases

Lung is most common site for thyroid cancer metastases

Look for pulmonary nodules, bone lesions (often lytic), and liver metastases. Brain mets are less common.

Carotid Artery Assessment

Evaluate for vessel encasement vs. displacement

>270° circumferential contact suggests encasement. Look for loss of fat plane and vessel wall irregularity.

Age-Related Staging

Remember the 55-year age cutoff for differentiated thyroid cancer

Patients <55 years have better prognosis and different stage groupings. This doesn't apply to medullary or anaplastic types.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Minimal vs. Gross Extrathyroidal Extension

Minimal extrathyroidal extension (microscopic) doesn't upstage T category. Only gross extension visible on imaging counts for T3 staging.

2
Forgetting Age Impact on Staging

Age ≥55 significantly affects overall stage grouping for differentiated thyroid cancers. A T3N1M0 tumor is Stage I if <55 years, but Stage II if ≥55 years.

3
Misidentifying Central vs. Lateral Nodes

Central compartment (Level VI) includes pretracheal, paratracheal, and prelaryngeal nodes. Don't confuse with lateral neck levels I-V.

4
Applying Age Criteria to All Histologies

Age-based staging only applies to papillary, follicular, and Hürthle cell carcinomas. Medullary and anaplastic carcinomas don't use age-based staging.

5
Underestimating Anaplastic Carcinoma

ALL anaplastic thyroid carcinomas are at least T4a. There are no T1-T3 anaplastic carcinomas. Intrathyroidal = T4a, extrathyroidal = T4b.

6
Missing Tracheoesophageal Groove Extension

Tumor extension into the tracheoesophageal groove may indicate recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement, which qualifies as T4a disease.

7
Confusing Vessel Contact vs. Encasement

Simple contact with vessels doesn't equal encasement. Look for >270° circumferential involvement and loss of fat planes for true encasement.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Patient Demographics

Note patient age and confirm histologic type from clinical history. Age ≥55 affects staging for differentiated thyroid cancers.

2
Primary Tumor Size

Measure maximum tumor dimension on any imaging plane. Use this for T1a (≤1cm), T1b (>1-2cm), T2 (>2-4cm), or T3 (>4cm) classification.

3
Thyroid Capsule Integrity

Assess for extrathyroidal extension. Look for capsule disruption, loss of fat planes, and extension into surrounding structures.

4
T4a Structure Assessment

Evaluate for invasion of subcutaneous tissue, strap muscles, larynx, trachea, esophagus, or recurrent laryngeal nerve.

5
T4b Structure Assessment

Look for prevertebral fascia invasion, carotid artery encasement, or mediastinal vessel involvement indicating unresectable disease.

6
Regional Lymph Node Evaluation

Systematically assess central compartment (Level VI) and lateral neck levels (I-V, VII). Measure largest pathologic node.

7
Distant Metastases Survey

Evaluate lungs (most common site), bones, liver, and brain for metastatic disease. Include upper mediastinum in field of view.

8
Final Stage Integration

Combine T, N, M stages with patient age and histology to determine overall stage. Remember age-based modifications for differentiated cancers.

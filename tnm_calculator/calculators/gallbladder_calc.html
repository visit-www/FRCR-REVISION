Use arterial, portal venous, and delayed phases for optimal assessment

Arterial phase: Vascular invasion assessment
Portal venous: Liver parenchyma and nodes
Delayed: Bile duct evaluation

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Mistaking Cholecystitis for Cancer

Acute cholecystitis can mimic gallbladder cancer with wall thickening. Look for gallstones, pericholecystic fluid, and clinical context. Cancer typically shows irregular, focal thickening with enhancement.

2
Underestimating T Stage

Don't rely solely on wall thickening. Carefully assess for liver invasion, peritoneal perforation, and adjacent organ involvement. Use multiple imaging planes and phases.

3
Missing Vascular Invasion

Subtle vascular invasion can be missed on portal venous phase alone. Always review arterial phase images for vessel encasement or deformity, especially portal vein and hepatic artery.

4
Confusing Regional vs Distant Nodes

Regional nodes include cystic duct, CBD, hepatic artery, and portal vein stations. Periaortic, pericaval, SMA, and celiac nodes are distant (M1), not regional (N1).

5
Overlooking Peritoneal Disease

Carefully examine the peritoneum for thickening, nodularity, or implants. Use lung windows to detect subtle peritoneal changes. Ascites with nodularity suggests M1 disease.

6
Size Criteria Confusion

T1 staging depends on size AND vascular invasion status. A 3cm tumor with vascular invasion is T2, not T1a. Always assess both parameters together.

7
Inadequate Liver Assessment

Don't just look at the gallbladder fossa. Examine the entire liver for metastases, especially segments IV and V. Use hepatocyte-specific contrast agents when available.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Gallbladder Wall Assessment

Measure wall thickness, assess for focal vs diffuse thickening, evaluate enhancement pattern, and look for intraluminal masses or polyps.

2
Tumor Size and Morphology

Measure maximum tumor dimension in any plane. Assess for solitary vs multiple lesions. Document tumor morphology (wall thickening, mass, replacement).

3
Liver Invasion Assessment

Examine gallbladder fossa for loss of fat plane, irregular liver contour, and direct tumor extension. Check segments IV and V carefully.

4
Vascular Invasion Evaluation

Assess portal vein and hepatic artery on arterial phase. Look for >50% circumferential contact, vessel deformity, or encasement.

5
Adjacent Organ Assessment

Evaluate duodenum, transverse colon, stomach, pancreas, and omentum for direct invasion. Look for loss of fat planes and enhancement.

6
Regional Lymph Node Evaluation

Assess cystic duct, common bile duct, hepatic artery, and portal vein node stations. Use size >1cm and morphologic criteria.

7
Peritoneal and Distant Disease

Examine peritoneum for thickening, nodularity, or implants. Check liver for metastases, lungs for deposits, and distant node stations.

8
Bile Duct Assessment

Evaluate for bile duct dilatation, wall thickening, or direct invasion. Check for jaundice-related findings and hilar involvement.

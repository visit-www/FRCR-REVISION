Key Imaging Tips

Vocal Cord Mobility Assessment

Critical for T staging - request phonation sequences if possible

Look for asymmetric cord movement, bulky tumor preventing mobility, or complete fixation. Impaired mobility suggests T2, fixation indicates T3.

Paraglottic Space Invasion

Key feature for T3 staging - look for fat stranding

Normal paraglottic fat should be symmetric and bright on T1. Any asymmetric soft tissue density or enhancement indicates invasion.

Cartilage Invasion Assessment

Distinguish sclerosis (T3) from through-and-through (T4a)

Inner cortex sclerosis/erosion = T3. Complete cartilage destruction with soft tissue extension = T4a. Use contrast-enhanced sequences.

Pre-epiglottic Space

Important for supraglottic tumors - fat obliteration indicates T3

Normal pre-epiglottic fat is bright on T1 and T2. Tumor replacement appears as soft tissue density with enhancement.

Subglottic Extension Measurement

Measure extension below glottis - ≥10mm = subglottic primary

Use coronal images to measure inferior extension from the level of the vocal cords. Important for staging and treatment planning.

Extranodal Extension (ENE)

Irregular nodal margins and surrounding fat stranding

Look for loss of sharp nodal margins, irregular enhancement extending into surrounding fat, and reactive changes in adjacent tissues.

Central Compartment Assessment

T4a feature - invasion of strap muscles, thyroid gland

Evaluate for tumor extension through cricothyroid membrane into strap muscles or direct thyroid gland invasion.

Carotid Encasement Criteria

T4b feature - >270° circumferential involvement

Measure circumferential involvement on axial images. >270° encasement indicates unresectability. Also look for vessel narrowing or occlusion.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing T1a vs T1b for Glottic Tumors

T1a involves one vocal cord, T1b involves both cords. This distinction affects treatment planning and prognosis. Always specify bilateral involvement.

2
Missing Paraglottic Space Invasion

Subtle fat stranding can be overlooked. Compare both sides carefully and use fat-suppressed sequences to highlight asymmetric enhancement.

3
Overcalling Cartilage Invasion

Age-related cartilage ossification can mimic invasion. Look for actual cortical disruption and soft tissue extension, not just enhancement.

4
Inadequate Subglottic Extension Assessment

Failing to measure subglottic extension can lead to incorrect primary site designation. Use coronal images for accurate measurement.

5
Misinterpreting Vocal Cord Mobility

Bulky tumor can mechanically impair mobility without true fixation. Correlate with clinical examination when possible.

6
Confusing Inner vs Outer Cortex Cartilage Invasion

Inner cortex erosion = T3, through-and-through = T4a. This distinction significantly affects staging and treatment approach.

7
Underestimating ENE in Small Nodes

Even small nodes can have ENE. Look carefully at nodal margins and surrounding fat, especially in nodes >1cm.

8
Missing Bilateral Nodal Disease

Carefully evaluate both sides of the neck. Even small contralateral nodes can upstage to N2c, significantly affecting prognosis.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Site and Subsite

Determine if tumor is supraglottic, glottic, or subglottic. This drives the entire staging approach and treatment planning.

2
Assess Vocal Cord Mobility

Review functional sequences if available. Look for asymmetric cord movement or complete fixation on phonation.

3
Evaluate for T4b Features First

Check for prevertebral space invasion, carotid encasement (>270°), and mediastinal involvement. These indicate unresectability.

4
Look for T4a Features

Assess cartilage invasion (through outer cortex), hyoid involvement, thyroid gland invasion, and central compartment extension.

5
Check T3 Criteria

Evaluate paraglottic space, pre-epiglottic space, inner cortex cartilage erosion, and vocal cord fixation.

6
Apply Subsite-Specific T1/T2 Criteria

Use appropriate criteria for the identified subsite. Remember T1a vs T1b distinction for glottic tumors.

7
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Measure largest node, count ipsilateral and contralateral nodes, assess for ENE. Check all nodal levels bilaterally.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate lung bases, liver, and bone for metastatic disease. Consider PET-CT correlation if available.

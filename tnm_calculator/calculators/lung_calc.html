Key Imaging Tips

Visceral Pleura Invasion

Look for pleural tags, puckering, or direct contact with pleura

The "pleural tag sign" - linear opacity extending from tumor to pleural surface. Visceral pleural invasion upgrades T1 tumors to T2a.

Chest Wall Invasion Assessment

Evaluate for rib destruction, soft tissue mass beyond pleura

>3cm contact with chest wall, obtuse angle with chest wall, or obvious rib destruction. Pain on palpation is clinical correlate.

Mediastinal Fat Plane Loss

Loss of fat plane suggests invasion but is not definitive

>90° contact with aorta, >3cm contact with mediastinum, or encasement of vessels suggests T4 disease. Consider cardiac MRI for equivocal cases.

Part-Solid Nodule Measurement

Measure only the solid component for staging

Ground-glass components don't count toward T staging. Use lung windows and measure the largest solid dimension on any plane.

Lymph Node Short Axis

Always measure short axis; >1cm is suspicious

Round nodes, central necrosis, or heterogeneous enhancement are additional suspicious features. PET-CT helps with metabolic activity assessment.

Separate Pulmonary Nodules

Location determines T3 vs T4 classification

Same lobe = T3, different ipsilateral lobe = T4, contralateral lobe = M1a. Must be confirmed as same histology to be considered satellite nodules.

Atelectasis vs Tumor

Distinguish collapsed lung from actual tumor mass

Atelectasis shows volume loss, air bronchograms, and enhances less than tumor. The "S-sign of Golden" suggests central mass with atelectasis.

Malignant Pleural Effusion

Cytology-positive effusion = M1a disease

Bloody, exudative effusion in lung cancer patient is suspicious. Requires cytological confirmation for M1a classification.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Measuring Ground-Glass Component

Only measure the solid component of part-solid nodules for T staging. Including ground-glass can lead to overstaging.

2
Missing Visceral Pleural Invasion

Subtle pleural tags or puckering can be easily missed. Always look for linear opacities extending to pleural surface from peripheral tumors.

3
Confusing Atelectasis with Tumor

Collapsed lung can mimic large tumor mass. Look for volume loss, air bronchograms, and the actual obstructing lesion.

4
Overinterpreting Mediastinal Contact

Simple contact doesn't equal invasion. Need >90° aortic contact, >3cm mediastinal contact, or clear fat plane loss for T4.

5
Wrong Lymph Node Measurement

Always measure short axis of lymph nodes, not long axis. Long axis measurements can lead to false positives.

6
Misclassifying Separate Nodules

Same lobe = T3, different ipsilateral lobe = T4, contralateral = M1a. Location matters significantly for staging.

7
Assuming All Effusions are Malignant

Pleural effusion needs cytological confirmation to be classified as M1a. Reactive effusions don't upstage.

8
Ignoring Size Thresholds

T2 features only apply to tumors ≤5cm. A 6cm tumor with visceral pleural invasion is T3, not T2b.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor

Locate the primary lesion, measure maximum dimension (solid component only for part-solid), and assess morphology.

2
Assess Local Invasion (T4)

Check for mediastinal, cardiac, great vessel, tracheal, esophageal, or vertebral invasion. These automatically make it T4.

3
Evaluate T3 Features

Look for chest wall invasion, phrenic nerve involvement, parietal pericardium invasion, or separate nodule in same lobe.

4
Check T2 Features

Assess for visceral pleural invasion, main bronchus involvement, or atelectasis extending to hilum (for tumors ≤5cm).

5
Apply Size Criteria

Use size thresholds: T1a(≤1cm), T1b(1-2cm), T1c(2-3cm), T2a(3-4cm), T2b(4-5cm), T3(5-7cm), T4(>7cm).

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Evaluate all nodal stations systematically. Measure short axis, assess distribution pattern (N1→N2→N3).

7
Search for Distant Disease

Check for pleural nodules, malignant effusion, contralateral lung nodules, and extrathoracic metastases.

8
Synthesize Final Stage

Combine T, N, M components using AJCC stage grouping table. Consider clinical implications for treatment planning.

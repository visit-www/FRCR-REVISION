Key Imaging Tips

Hypopharynx Anatomy

Three subsites: pyriform sinuses, postcricoid area, and posterior pharyngeal wall

Use axial and coronal planes to assess all three subsites systematically

T3 Epiglottis Extension

Look for tumor extending to the lingual (superior) surface of the epiglottis

Sagittal images are crucial for assessing epiglottic involvement

Laryngeal Invasion (T4a)

Assess for invasion through the laryngeal framework or vocal cord paralysis

Vocal cord paralysis may indicate recurrent laryngeal nerve invasion

Carotid Encasement (T4b)

Look for >270° circumferential contact or vessel irregularity

Use contrast-enhanced CT or MR angiography for accurate assessment

ENE Assessment

Look for irregular nodal borders, fat stranding, or adjacent structure invasion

ENE upstages any N category to N3b - critical for prognosis

Node Measurement

Measure short-axis diameter, not long-axis

Short-axis >1cm in levels II-IV suggests metastatic involvement

Skull Base Invasion

Assess for bone erosion or marrow signal changes on MRI

Coronal images best demonstrate skull base involvement

Pterygoid Assessment

Distinguish medial (T4a) from lateral (T4b) pterygoid muscle invasion

Axial images at the level of the maxillary sinuses are key

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing medial vs lateral pterygoid muscles

Medial pterygoid invasion = T4a (resectable), lateral pterygoid = T4b (unresectable). The medial pterygoid is closer to the mandible, lateral pterygoid attaches to the skull base.

2
Missing epiglottic extension for T3

Any extension to the lingual surface of epiglottis = T3, regardless of size. Don't forget to check sagittal images for subtle epiglottic involvement.

3
Understaging ENE-positive nodes

ANY extranodal extension automatically makes it N3b, regardless of size or number. ENE has profound prognostic significance in hypopharynx cancer.

4
Measuring long-axis instead of short-axis

Always measure the short-axis diameter of lymph nodes for staging. Long-axis measurements can be misleading and are not used in TNM staging.

5
Overcalling carotid encasement

Carotid encasement requires >270° circumferential contact or vessel irregularity. Simple contact or <270° involvement is not encasement.

6
Forgetting to assess all hypopharynx subsites

Systematically evaluate pyriform sinuses, postcricoid area, and posterior pharyngeal wall. Tumors can be multifocal or extend between subsites.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Site

Locate the primary tumor within hypopharynx subsites: pyriform sinus, postcricoid area, or posterior pharyngeal wall

2
Measure Maximum Dimension

Measure the largest tumor dimension in any plane for T1-T3 size criteria (≤2cm, >2-4cm, >4cm)

3
Assess T4b Features First

Check for unresectable features: lateral pterygoid, pterygoid plates, lateral nasopharynx, skull base, carotid encasement

4
Assess T4a Features

Look for resectable invasion: larynx, deep tongue muscles, medial pterygoid, hard palate, mandible

5
Check Epiglottic Extension

Assess for extension to lingual surface of epiglottis (T3 feature) using sagittal images

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Evaluate all nodal levels, measure short-axis diameter of largest node, count ipsilateral and contralateral nodes

7
ENE Evaluation

Look for extranodal extension signs: irregular borders, fat stranding, adjacent structure invasion

8
Distant Metastases Survey

Assess for distant metastases in lungs, liver, bones, and other distant sites

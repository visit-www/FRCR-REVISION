T Stage Reference Table

T Stage Nasal Cavity/Ethmoid Maxillary Sinus
T1 Single subsite, ± bony invasion Limited to mucosa, no bone erosion
T2 Two subsites or adjacent region, ± bony invasion Bone erosion/destruction, hard palate/middle meatus (excludes posterior wall/pterygoid plates)
T3 Medial wall/floor of orbit, maxillary sinus, palate, or cribriform plate

Key Imaging Tips

Bone Window Assessment

Always review bone windows to assess for subtle bone erosion, especially at the cribriform plate and orbital walls.

Use thin-section CT with bone algorithm for optimal bone detail

Orbital Involvement

Distinguish between periorbital fat invasion (T4a) and orbital apex involvement (T4b).

Coronal images are crucial for assessing orbital floor and medial wall

Pterygoid Plates

Pterygoid plate involvement indicates T4a. Look for cortical destruction or replacement by tumor.

Axial images best demonstrate pterygoid plate integrity

Intracranial Extension

T4b requires invasion beyond the skull base. Look for dural enhancement or brain parenchymal involvement.

MRI with gadolinium superior for assessing dural involvement

Cribriform Plate

Cribriform plate involvement can be T3 or T4a depending on extent. Assess for olfactory fossa involvement.

Sagittal reformats help assess anterior skull base involvement

Nodal Assessment

Measure nodes in the largest dimension. Look for loss of fatty hilum, round shape, and extranodal extension.

Contrast-enhanced CT or MRI essential for ENE assessment

Subsite Differentiation

Accurately identify whether tumor arises from nasal cavity/ethmoid vs maxillary sinus as T1/T2 criteria differ.

Coronal images best show anatomical boundaries between subsites

Perineural Spread

Look for abnormal enhancement along cranial nerve pathways, particularly V2 (maxillary division of trigeminal).

MRI with fat suppression sequences optimal for perineural spread detection

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing T3 vs T4a Cribriform Involvement

Cribriform plate invasion alone is T3, but if there's extension to anterior cranial fossa, it becomes T4a. Look carefully at the extent of involvement.

2
Missing Subtle Bone Erosion

Small areas of bone destruction can be easily missed on soft tissue windows. Always review bone windows systematically.

3
Overestimating Orbital Involvement

Periorbital edema or inflammatory changes don't constitute invasion. Look for actual tumor tissue crossing anatomical boundaries.

4
Clinical vs Pathological N Staging Confusion

Remember that pN2a includes small nodes with ENE, while cN2a requires larger nodes without ENE. The criteria differ significantly.

5
Inadequate Assessment of ENE

Extranodal extension requires careful evaluation of nodal margins. Look for irregular borders, fat stranding, and loss of fascial planes.

6
Misidentifying Primary Subsite

The T1/T2 criteria are completely different for nasal cavity/ethmoid vs maxillary sinus. Accurate subsite identification is crucial.

7
Underestimating Pterygoid Involvement

Pterygoid plate or pterygoid fossa involvement automatically makes it T4a. Don't miss this important landmark.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Subsite

Determine if tumor arises from nasal cavity/ethmoid complex or maxillary sinus. This affects T1/T2 criteria.

2
Assess for T4b Features First

Look for brain, dura, orbital apex, middle cranial fossa, or clivus involvement. If present, automatically T4b.

3
Check for T4a Features

Evaluate anterior orbital contents, skin, pterygoid plates, infratemporal fossa, and sphenoid/frontal sinuses.

4
Evaluate T3 Criteria

Look for invasion of medial orbital wall, orbital floor, maxillary sinus, palate, or cribriform plate.

5
Apply T1/T2 Subsite-Specific Criteria

Use appropriate criteria based on primary subsite identified in step 1. Nasal/ethmoid uses subsite number, maxillary uses bone erosion.

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Measure largest node, count ipsilateral/contralateral/bilateral nodes, and assess for extranodal extension.

7
Apply Appropriate N Staging

Use clinical (cN) or pathological (pN) criteria as appropriate. Remember the differences in N2a and N3b definitions.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate for distant metastases in lungs, liver, bone, and other organs. Any distant disease = M1.

Key Imaging Tips

Cartilage Invasion Assessment

Distinguish between inner and outer cortex invasion

Inner cortex invasion = T3, Outer cortex invasion = T4a. Look for sclerosis, erosion, or extralaryngeal extension on CT. MRI shows loss of low signal intensity on T1.

Paraglottic Space Evaluation

Critical space for T3 staging

Paraglottic space is bounded by thyroid cartilage laterally and laryngeal ventricle medially. Invasion indicates T3 disease and affects surgical planning.

Pre-epiglottic Space Assessment

Important for supraglottic tumors

Space between epiglottis and thyroid cartilage/hyoid bone. Best seen on sagittal images. Invasion indicates T3 disease and may require more extensive surgery.

Vocal Cord Mobility

Clinical correlation essential

Fixed vocal cord = T3. Imaging shows arytenoid cartilage fixation, cricoarytenoid joint involvement, or posterior glottic extension. Always correlate with laryngoscopy findings.

Nodal Short Axis Measurement

Measure perpendicular to long axis

Use short axis diameter for nodal staging. Round nodes >1cm or oval nodes >1.5cm short axis are suspicious. Central necrosis and irregular enhancement suggest malignancy.

Extranodal Extension (ENE)

Automatic N3b regardless of size

Look for irregular nodal borders, fat stranding, invasion of adjacent structures. ENE significantly impacts prognosis and treatment planning.

Subglottic Extension

Measure distance from vocal cords

Extension >1cm below vocal cords may require tracheostomy. Important for surgical planning and staging of glottic tumors.

Thyroid Gland Invasion

T4a feature - look carefully

Direct invasion through cricothyroid membrane. May require thyroidectomy. Look for loss of fat plane and direct tumor extension into thyroid parenchyma.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Inner vs Outer Cartilage Invasion

Inner cortex invasion is T3, outer cortex is T4a. Carefully assess the extent of cartilage involvement on both CT and MRI. Sclerosis alone may not indicate invasion.

2
Missing Paraglottic Space Invasion

This small space is easily overlooked but critical for T3 staging. Use axial images at the level of the vocal cords and look for fat replacement or mass effect.

3
Inadequate Nodal Measurement

Always measure short axis diameter, not long axis. Include all visible nodes in your count, not just the largest. Document bilateral vs unilateral distribution.

4
Overlooking Extranodal Extension

ENE automatically makes any node N3b regardless of size. Look for irregular borders, fat stranding, and invasion of adjacent muscles or vessels.

5
Misinterpreting Post-Treatment Changes

Previous radiation or surgery can cause fibrosis, edema, and cartilage changes that mimic tumor invasion. Always correlate with clinical history and prior imaging.

6
Ignoring Subsite-Specific Staging

T1-T3 criteria differ between supraglottis, glottis, and subglottis. A tumor crossing subsites may change the staging criteria applied.

7
Underestimating Thyroid Cartilage Ossification

Ossified cartilage appears as cortical bone on CT. Don't mistake normal ossification for invasion. Look for actual cortical disruption or soft tissue extension.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor Location

Determine if tumor is supraglottic, glottic, or subglottic. Note if it crosses anatomical boundaries as this affects staging criteria.

2
Assess Local Extent

Evaluate tumor size and local spread. Check for involvement of adjacent subsites, vocal cord mobility, and extension beyond the larynx.

3
Evaluate Cartilage Involvement

Systematically assess thyroid, cricoid, and arytenoid cartilages. Distinguish between inner cortex (T3) and outer cortex (T4a) invasion.

4
Check Critical Spaces

Examine paraglottic and pre-epiglottic spaces. Invasion of these spaces indicates T3 disease and affects surgical approach.

5
Assess for T4a Features

Look for invasion of thyroid gland, esophagus, hyoid bone, or central compartment soft tissues. These indicate moderately advanced disease.

6
Evaluate Regional Lymph Nodes

Systematically assess all nodal levels. Measure short axis diameter, count nodes, and assess for extranodal extension.

7
Search for Distant Metastases

Check lungs, liver, bones, and other distant sites. Include assessment of the chest if staging CT was performed.

8
Correlate with Clinical Findings

Review laryngoscopy findings, especially vocal cord mobility. Correlate imaging findings with clinical examination and prior studies.

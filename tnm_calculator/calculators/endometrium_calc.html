💡 Key Imaging Tips

MRI for Myometrial Assessment

T2-weighted images best show myometrial invasion depth for adenosarcomas.

Use junctional zone thinning and signal intensity changes to assess invasion depth accurately.

Size Measurement Protocol

Measure maximum tumor dimension on any imaging plane for LMS/ESS staging.

Include all tumor components, not just the dominant mass. Use multiplanar reconstruction if needed.

Adnexal Involvement Detection

Look for direct extension or separate tumor deposits in ovaries/tubes.

Distinguish from reactive changes. Solid components with restricted diffusion suggest malignancy.

Abdominal Tissue Assessment

Systematically evaluate peritoneal surfaces, omentum, and bowel serosa.

Count discrete sites of involvement to distinguish T3a (single) from T3b (multiple sites).

Lymph Node Evaluation

Assess pelvic and para-aortic nodes using size and morphologic criteria.

PET/CT improves specificity for nodal staging. Consider metabolically active nodes regardless of size.

Distant Metastasis Screening

Common sites include lungs, liver, and bones for endometrial sarcomas.

Chest CT is essential. Consider bone scan or PET/CT for comprehensive staging in high-grade tumors.

Bladder/Rectal Invasion

T4 requires mucosal invasion, not just serosal contact or mass effect.

Look for loss of fat plane, mucosal irregularity, or intraluminal extension on MRI.

Contrast Enhancement Patterns

Sarcomas often show heterogeneous enhancement with necrotic areas.

Dynamic contrast enhancement helps differentiate from benign leiomyomas and assess vascularity.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Staging Systems

Adenosarcomas use myometrial invasion criteria (like endometrial carcinoma), while LMS/ESS use size criteria. Don't mix the systems - always check the histologic subtype first.

2
Misinterpreting T4 Criteria

T4 requires actual mucosal invasion of bladder or rectum, not just mass effect or serosal involvement. Look for loss of mucosal integrity, not just contact.

3
Incorrect Abdominal Site Counting

For T3 staging, count discrete anatomical sites of abdominal involvement. Multiple deposits in the same organ count as one site, while involvement of different organs counts as multiple sites.

4
Overlooking Size Thresholds

The 5 cm cutoff for LMS/ESS T1 staging is critical. Measure the maximum dimension carefully on any imaging plane - don't rely on axial images alone.

5
Misclassifying Adnexal Involvement

Adnexal involvement (T2a) includes both ovaries and fallopian tubes. Don't forget to evaluate the tubes, especially in cases with complex pelvic anatomy.

6
Inadequate Myometrial Assessment

For adenosarcomas, accurately measuring myometrial invasion depth is crucial. Use high-resolution T2-weighted MRI and measure perpendicular to the endometrial-myometrial junction.

7
Ignoring Regional Node Definition

Regional nodes for uterine sarcomas include pelvic and para-aortic nodes. Don't limit assessment to pelvic nodes only - para-aortic involvement is still N1, not M1.

8
Confusing M1 vs T2/T3 Disease

Adnexal, pelvic, and abdominal tissue involvement are T2/T3, not M1. Only truly distant sites (lung, liver, bone, etc.) qualify as M1 disease.

🔍 8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Histologic Subtype

Identify whether you're dealing with adenosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, or endometrial stromal sarcoma. This determines which T-stage criteria to apply.

2
Assess Primary Tumor Location

Determine if the tumor is limited to the uterus (T1) or extends beyond. Look for clear margins and assess relationship to uterine contours.

3
Measure Tumor Dimensions

For LMS/ESS: measure maximum dimension on any plane. For adenosarcomas: assess myometrial invasion depth as percentage of total myometrial thickness.

4
Evaluate Pelvic Extension

Systematically assess adnexa (ovaries, fallopian tubes) and other pelvic structures (parametria, vagina, cervix) for tumor involvement.

5
Check for Abdominal Spread

Examine peritoneal surfaces, omentum, bowel serosa, and other abdominal structures. Count discrete sites of involvement for T3a vs T3b classification.

6
Assess Bladder and Rectal Invasion

Look specifically for mucosal invasion (T4), not just mass effect. Evaluate fat planes and mucosal integrity on high-resolution images.

7
Evaluate Regional Lymph Nodes

Assess both pelvic and para-aortic lymph nodes using size criteria (>1 cm short axis) and morphologic features. Consider PET activity if available.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Systematically evaluate lungs, liver, bones, and other distant sites. Remember that adnexal/pelvic/abdominal involvement is not M1 disease.

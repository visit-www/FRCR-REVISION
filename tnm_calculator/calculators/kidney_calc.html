Key Imaging Tips

IVC Thrombus Assessment

Use coronal and sagittal reformats to assess cranial extent of tumor thrombus

Look for enhancement within thrombus to differentiate tumor thrombus from bland thrombus. Tumor thrombus enhances similar to primary tumor.

Gerota's Fascia Evaluation

Assess for tumor extension beyond the thin fascial layer surrounding perirenal fat

Gerota's fascia appears as a thin line on CT/MRI. T4 requires extension beyond this fascia into retroperitoneal fat or adjacent organs.

Renal Vein Thrombus

Evaluate renal vein on venous phase imaging for filling defects

Tumor thrombus typically enhances and may expand the vein. Use multiplanar imaging to assess extent into IVC.

Perirenal Fat Invasion

Look for stranding or nodular densities in perirenal fat

T3a includes invasion of perirenal or renal sinus fat but must remain within Gerota's fascia. Use fat-suppressed sequences on MRI.

Adrenal Gland Assessment

Evaluate for direct tumor extension vs. separate adrenal metastasis

Direct extension (T4) shows continuity with primary tumor. Separate adrenal metastasis is M1 disease.

Pelvicalyceal System

Assess for tumor invasion into collecting system

T3a includes invasion of pelvicalyceal system. Look for filling defects or wall thickening on excretory phase imaging.

Regional Node Assessment

Evaluate hilar, paracaval, paraaortic, and interaortocaval nodes

Size criteria (>1 cm short axis) and morphology (round, heterogeneous) suggest metastatic involvement.

Pseudocapsule Recognition

Identify the fibrous pseudocapsule around RCC on T2-weighted MRI

Intact pseudocapsule suggests T1/T2 disease. Disruption may indicate T3a with perirenal fat invasion.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Tumor Thrombus with Bland Thrombus

Tumor thrombus enhances and may expand the vessel, while bland thrombus does not enhance. Always assess enhancement pattern on post-contrast imaging.

2
Misidentifying Gerota's Fascia

Gerota's fascia is the thin outer layer, not the renal capsule. Extension beyond Gerota's fascia (not just the renal capsule) defines T4 disease.

3
Incorrect IVC Thrombus Level Assessment

Use coronal imaging to accurately assess cranial extent. T3b is below diaphragm, T3c is above diaphragm or involves IVC wall.

4
Overlooking Renal Sinus Fat Invasion

T3a includes renal sinus fat invasion, which can be subtle. Look for loss of normal fat planes around the collecting system.

5
Adrenal Involvement Misclassification

Direct extension to adrenal is T4, but separate adrenal metastasis is M1. Assess for continuity vs. separate lesion.

6
Inadequate Venous Phase Imaging

Venous phase is crucial for assessing vascular invasion. Arterial phase alone may miss renal vein or IVC thrombus.

7
Size Measurement Errors

Measure the largest dimension in any plane, not just axial. Use multiplanar reconstructions for accurate sizing.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify and Measure Primary Tumor

Locate the primary renal mass and measure maximum dimension in any plane. Note enhancement pattern and internal characteristics.

2
Assess Renal Capsule and Pseudocapsule

Evaluate for breach of renal capsule and integrity of fibrous pseudocapsule, particularly on T2-weighted MRI sequences.

3
Evaluate Perirenal and Renal Sinus Fat

Look for stranding, nodularity, or mass effect in perirenal fat. Assess renal sinus fat for invasion around collecting system.

4
Trace Renal Vein and IVC

Follow renal vein from hilum to IVC. Assess for tumor thrombus using venous phase imaging. Evaluate IVC extent with coronal views.

5
Examine Gerota's Fascia

Identify the thin fascial boundary and assess for tumor extension beyond this layer into retroperitoneal fat or adjacent organs.

6
Evaluate Adrenal Glands

Assess both adrenal glands for direct extension (T4) vs. separate metastatic deposits (M1). Look for continuity with primary tumor.

7
Survey Regional Lymph Nodes

Evaluate hilar, paracaval, paraaortic, and interaortocaval lymph nodes for size, morphology, and enhancement characteristics.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Assess lungs, liver, bones, and other organs for metastatic disease. Include evaluation of contralateral kidney and adrenal.

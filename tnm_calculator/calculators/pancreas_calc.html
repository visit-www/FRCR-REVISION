Key Imaging Tips

Optimal CT Protocol

Use thin-section (2-3mm) multiphasic CT with pancreatic parenchymal phase and portal venous phase

Pancreatic phase: 40-50 seconds post-contrast. Portal phase: 70-80 seconds.

Vascular Assessment

Evaluate arterial encasement >180° circumference for T4 staging

Use curved MPR and axial images. Look for loss of fat plane and vessel contour irregularity.

Size Measurement

Measure maximum tumor dimension on any imaging plane

Include both solid and cystic components. Use soft tissue windows for accurate measurement.

Regional Nodes

Include pancreaticoduodenal, pyloric, hepatic, splenic, and celiac nodes

Size >1cm suspicious. Look for central necrosis and irregular enhancement.

Liver Metastases

Pancreatic adenocarcinoma metastases are typically hypodense on portal phase

Small lesions may be isodense. Consider MRI with DWI for problem-solving.

Peritoneal Disease

Look for ascites, omental nodules, and peritoneal thickening

Check paracolic gutters, pelvis, and lesser sac. Even small volume ascites can be significant.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Mistaking pancreatitis for tumor

Post-procedural pancreatitis can mimic or obscure tumor. Always image before biopsy or stent placement when possible.

2
Underestimating vascular involvement

Subtle arterial encasement may be missed. Use thin sections and multiplanar reconstructions to assess vessel circumference involvement.

3
Missing small liver metastases

Small hepatic metastases may be isodense on CT. Consider MRI with DWI for better detection of small lesions.

4
Incorrect size measurement

Measure the entire tumor including cystic components, not just the solid portion. Use the largest dimension on any plane.

5
Overlooking peritoneal disease

Subtle peritoneal implants can be missed. Carefully evaluate all peritoneal surfaces, especially in the pelvis and paracolic gutters.

6
Confusing reactive vs. metastatic nodes

Size alone is not reliable. Look for irregular enhancement, central necrosis, and clustering. Consider location relative to primary tumor.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor Location

Determine if tumor is in pancreatic head, body, or tail. This affects surgical approach and prognosis.

2
Measure Maximum Tumor Dimension

Use calipers on the largest dimension in any plane. Include both solid and cystic components.

3
Assess Arterial Involvement

Evaluate celiac axis, SMA, and common hepatic artery for encasement >180° circumference.

4
Evaluate Venous Involvement

Check portal vein, SMV, and splenic vein for narrowing, occlusion, or contour irregularity.

5
Count Regional Lymph Nodes

Identify and count suspicious regional nodes. Include pancreaticoduodenal, pyloric, hepatic, splenic, and celiac groups.

6
Search for Liver Metastases

Systematically evaluate all liver segments. Look for hypodense lesions on portal phase imaging.

7
Assess Peritoneal Surfaces

Check for ascites, omental nodules, and peritoneal thickening. Include pelvis and paracolic gutters.

8
Look for Other Distant Disease

Evaluate lungs, bones, and other organs for metastatic disease. Check for enlarged distant lymph nodes.

⚖️ Clinical vs Pathological T Staging

Stage Clinical (cT) Pathological (pT)
T1 Psum ≤12mm + Fmax ≤5mm Limited to ipsilateral parietal pleura, no fissure involvement
T2 Psum ≤12mm + Fmax >5mm OR Psum 12-30mm ± additional features Fissure, lung parenchyma, or non-transmural diaphragm invasion
T3 Psum >30mm ± additional features Mediastinal fat, pericardium surface, endothoracic fascia, or solitary chest wall soft tissue
T4 Chest wall bony, mediastinal organs, diffuse chest wall, transdiaphragmatic, contralateral pleura, malignant effusion Same as clinical + transmural diaphragm/pericardium invasion

💡 Key Imaging Tips

Pleural Thickness Measurement

Measure pleural thickness at 3 prespecified levels on axial CT images

Use soft tissue windows and measure perpendicular to pleural surface

Fissure Involvement Assessment

Carefully evaluate fissural pleura for tumor involvement

Fmax >5mm in clinical staging affects T stage classification

Chest Wall Invasion

Distinguish between soft tissue and bony chest wall invasion

Bony invasion = T4, soft tissue invasion may be T2/T3 depending on extent

Mediastinal Fat Assessment

Look for tumor extension into mediastinal fat planes

Loss of fat planes suggests invasion - correlate with pathology when available

Diaphragmatic Involvement

Assess for diaphragm muscle invasion vs. transmural extension

Transmural invasion (through to peritoneum) = T4

Pericardial Assessment

Evaluate pericardial involvement and presence of effusion

Malignant pericardial effusion = T4; surface involvement may be T3

Lymph Node Mapping

Use standard thoracic lymph node station maps

N1 = ipsilateral intrathoracic; N2 = contralateral mediastinal or supraclavicular

Contralateral Pleural Extension

Carefully assess for tumor crossing to contralateral pleura

Any contralateral pleural involvement = T4

⚠️ Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Clinical vs Pathological Staging

Clinical staging uses pleural thickness measurements and imaging criteria, while pathological staging relies on histologic invasion patterns. Don't apply pathological criteria to clinical staging.

2
Incorrect Pleural Thickness Measurement

Pleural thickness should be measured at 3 specific levels and summed (Psum). Don't use maximum thickness alone or measure at random locations.

3
Missing Fissure Involvement

Fissure involvement (Fmax >5mm) can upgrade T1 to T2 in clinical staging. Always assess fissural pleura carefully on thin-section CT.

4
Misclassifying Chest Wall Invasion

Solitary soft tissue chest wall invasion may be T2/T3, but bony invasion or diffuse involvement is always T4. Distinguish carefully between these patterns.

5
Overlooking Malignant Pericardial Effusion

Any malignant pericardial effusion automatically makes the tumor T4, regardless of other features. Don't forget to assess pericardial fluid cytology.

6
Incorrect Lymph Node Classification

N1 includes all ipsilateral intrathoracic nodes (not just hilar). N2 is contralateral mediastinal or any supraclavicular nodes. Don't use lung cancer N staging criteria.

7
Underestimating Mediastinal Invasion

Mediastinal fat invasion can be subtle on imaging. Look for loss of fat planes and correlate with clinical findings. This affects T staging significantly.

🔍 8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Diagnosis

Verify pleural mesothelioma diagnosis and determine if clinical or pathological staging is appropriate based on available information.

2
Assess Pleural Thickness

For clinical staging, measure pleural thickness at 3 prespecified levels on axial CT and calculate Psum. Use soft tissue windows for accuracy.

3
Evaluate Fissure Involvement

Assess fissural pleura for tumor involvement and measure maximum fissure involvement (Fmax). This affects clinical T staging.

4
Check for T4 Features

Systematically look for unresectable features: chest wall bony invasion, mediastinal organs, diffuse chest wall, transdiaphragmatic extension, contralateral pleura, malignant pericardial effusion.

5
Assess Local Invasion

Evaluate for mediastinal fat, pericardial surface, endothoracic fascia, and chest wall soft tissue invasion. These affect T2/T3 classification.

6
Map Lymph Nodes

Identify involved lymph node stations. N1 = ipsilateral intrathoracic nodes; N2 = contralateral mediastinal or supraclavicular nodes.

7
Screen for Metastases

Systematically evaluate for distant metastases in liver, bone, brain, and other organs. Any distant metastasis = M1 = Stage IV.

8
Synthesize and Stage

Combine T, N, and M classifications to determine overall stage. Consider clinical implications and treatment options based on final stage.

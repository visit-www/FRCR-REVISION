Key Imaging Tips

CT Urography Protocol

Use triphasic CT with nephrographic, excretory phases for optimal visualization

Excretory phase at 8-10 minutes shows filling defects best

MR Urography Advantages

Superior soft tissue contrast, no radiation, useful in renal impairment

T2-weighted sequences show urine as bright signal

T3 Assessment

Look for fat stranding around renal pelvis or ureter on CT

Different criteria for renal pelvis vs ureter location

Multifocal Disease

Image entire urinary tract - synchronous tumors common

Stage each tumor separately if multiple sites involved

Regional Lymph Nodes

Hilar, paracaval, para-aortic, common iliac nodes are regional

Size criteria less reliable than morphology

Enhancement Patterns

Urothelial tumors show early enhancement on arterial phase

Filling defects on excretory phase are key finding

Hydronephrosis Assessment

Degree of obstruction doesn't correlate with T stage

Focus on depth of invasion, not obstruction severity

Retrograde Pyelography

May be needed if excretory phase inadequate

Better delineation of tumor extent in non-functioning kidney

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing T3 criteria by location

T3 for renal pelvis includes parenchymal invasion, but T3 for ureter is periureteral fat only. Don't apply the same criteria to both locations.

2
Overstaging based on hydronephrosis

Severe hydronephrosis doesn't automatically mean higher T stage. Focus on actual depth of tumor invasion, not degree of obstruction.

3
Missing synchronous tumors

Always image the entire urinary tract. Up to 30% of patients have synchronous bladder tumors. Each tumor should be staged separately.

4
Inadequate excretory phase imaging

Don't rely only on nephrographic phase. Excretory phase at 8-10 minutes is crucial for detecting filling defects and tumor extent.

5
Confusing clinical vs pathological N staging

N1a and N1b subcategories only apply to pathological staging. Clinical staging uses simpler N0/N1 classification.

6
Misidentifying regional lymph nodes

Regional nodes include hilar, paracaval, para-aortic, and common iliac. Inguinal or mediastinal nodes are distant metastases (M1).

7
Overlooking in-transit metastases

Look for discontinuous tumor deposits between primary and regional nodes. These are classified as N2 (without nodal mets) or N3 (with nodal mets).

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Protocol Check

Ensure adequate CT urography with nephrographic and excretory phases. Confirm contrast timing and patient preparation.

2
Identify Primary Tumor

Locate the primary tumor and determine if it's in renal pelvis or ureter. Look for filling defects, wall thickening, or masses.

3
Assess Depth of Invasion

Determine T stage by evaluating invasion depth: subepithelial (T1), muscularis (T2), beyond muscularis (T3), or adjacent organs (T4).

4
Survey Entire Urinary Tract

Check for synchronous tumors in contralateral system, ipsilateral ureter/pelvis, and bladder. Stage each separately if present.

5
Evaluate Regional Lymph Nodes

Assess hilar, paracaval, para-aortic, and common iliac nodes. Look for size, morphology, and enhancement patterns.

6
Check for In-transit Disease

Look for discontinuous tumor deposits between primary and regional nodes along lymphatic pathways.

7
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate lungs, liver, bone, and distant lymph nodes. Consider additional imaging if clinically indicated.

8
Synthesize Final Stage

Combine T, N, M components using AJCC staging groups. Consider clinical implications and treatment planning needs.

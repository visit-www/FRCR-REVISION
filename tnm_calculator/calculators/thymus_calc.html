Key Imaging Tips

Contrast Enhancement Patterns

Thymic masses typically show heterogeneous enhancement. Look for areas of necrosis or cystic change.

Use both arterial and venous phases to assess vascular invasion

Fat Plane Assessment

Loss of fat planes suggests invasion but can be inflammatory. Correlate with enhancement patterns.

Preserved fat planes generally exclude invasion

Vascular Invasion Criteria

Look for vessel encasement >180°, luminal narrowing, or complete occlusion.

Distinguish intrapericardial vs extrapericardial pulmonary vessels

Pericardial vs Pleural Invasion

Pericardial invasion = T2. Pleural nodules = M1a. Don't confuse the two.

Pericardial thickening/enhancement suggests invasion

Chest Wall Invasion

Look for rib destruction, intercostal muscle invasion, or soft tissue extension.

MRI may be superior for chest wall assessment

Nodal Assessment

Thymic tumors have low rates of nodal metastases. Size criteria less reliable than morphology.

Use ITMIG/IASLC thymic nodal map for classification

Myasthenia Gravis Association

More common with B1, B2, B3 thymomas. May affect surgical planning.

Clinical history important for staging implications

Multiplanar Reconstruction

Use coronal and sagittal planes to assess craniocaudal extent and vascular relationships.

Particularly helpful for great vessel assessment

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Pericardial vs Pleural Involvement

Pericardial invasion is T2, while pleural nodules are M1a. Carefully distinguish between pericardial thickening/invasion and separate pleural deposits.

2
Missing Intrapericardial vs Extrapericardial Distinction

Intrapericardial pulmonary vessel invasion is T4, while extrapericardial is T3. Know the pericardial reflections around pulmonary vessels.

3
Overlooking Size Criteria for T1

T1a vs T1b distinction is based on 5cm cutoff. Don't forget to measure maximum dimension when no invasion is present.

4
Misclassifying Mediastinal Fat Invasion

Invasion into mediastinal fat alone is still T1, not T2. Only becomes T2 with pericardium, lung, or phrenic nerve invasion.

5
Inadequate Nodal Station Assessment

Use the ITMIG/IASLC nodal map. Anterior nodes are N1, deep nodes are N2. Location matters more than size for thymic tumors.

6
Confusing Inflammatory Changes with Invasion

Post-biopsy or inflammatory changes can mimic invasion. Correlate with clinical history and enhancement patterns.

7
Missing Arch Vessel Involvement

Carefully assess all arch vessels (brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid, left subclavian). Any involvement makes it T4.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Thymic Location

Verify mass is in prevascular mediastinum. Check for normal thymic tissue remnants.

2
Measure Maximum Dimension

Measure in any plane for T1a vs T1b distinction (5cm cutoff).

3
Assess Local Invasion (T2 structures)

Check pericardium, lung, and phrenic nerve for direct invasion.

4
Evaluate Vascular Invasion (T3 structures)

Assess brachiocephalic vein, SVC, chest wall, extrapericardial pulmonary vessels.

5
Check Major Vessel/Organ Invasion (T4)

Examine aorta, arch vessels, intrapericardial pulmonary vessels, myocardium, trachea, esophagus.

6
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Use ITMIG nodal map. Assess anterior (N1) and deep (N2) nodal stations.

7
Search for Metastases

Look for pleural/pericardial nodules (M1a) and pulmonary/extrathoracic metastases (M1b).

8
Correlate with Clinical History

Note myasthenia gravis, prior treatments, and histologic subtype if known.

Key Imaging Tips

Measure Invasive Component Only

For T staging, measure only the invasive carcinoma component, not the total extent including DCIS.

DCIS extent is important for surgical planning but not TNM staging

Multifocal Disease Staging

Use the largest invasive focus for T stage determination. Document multifocality separately.

Multiple foci don't change T stage but affect treatment planning

Chest Wall vs Pectoral Muscle

T4a requires invasion of chest wall structures (ribs, intercostal muscles), not just pectoral muscle.

Pectoral muscle invasion alone does not qualify as T4a

Skin Involvement Assessment

T4b requires skin ulceration, satellite nodules, or edema. Reactive skin thickening is not T4b.

Look for direct tumor involvement, not just inflammatory changes

Internal Mammary Node Significance

IM nodes >5mm short axis are significant. Distinguish sentinel biopsy detection from clinical detection.

Clinical detection (imaging/exam) has different staging implications than sentinel node detection

Inflammatory Carcinoma Diagnosis

T4d requires clinical findings of erythema and edema involving ≥1/3 of breast skin.

Pathologic confirmation of dermal lymphatic invasion supports but is not required for diagnosis

Node Size vs Number

N staging is primarily based on number of positive nodes, not size. Document both for completeness.

Size helps assess likelihood of positivity but number determines N stage

Micrometastasis Recognition

Micrometastases (>0.2-2mm) have different staging implications than macrometastases (>2mm).

Usually determined histologically but may be suggested by very small positive nodes

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Including DCIS in Size Measurement

Only measure the invasive component for T staging. DCIS extent is documented separately but doesn't affect TNM stage.

2
Confusing Pectoral Muscle with Chest Wall

Pectoral muscle invasion alone is not T4a. True chest wall invasion requires involvement of ribs, intercostal muscles, or serratus anterior.

3
Missing Internal Mammary Nodes

Always evaluate internal mammary chain. Nodes >5mm short axis are significant and affect N staging.

4
Misinterpreting Skin Changes

Distinguish between reactive skin thickening (not T4b) and true tumor involvement with ulceration or satellite nodules (T4b).

5
Overlooking Inflammatory Carcinoma

T4d requires clinical findings of erythema and edema involving ≥1/3 of breast. Don't rely solely on pathology for diagnosis.

6
Incorrect N2 vs N3 Classification

N2 is 4-9 axillary nodes OR clinically detected IM. N3 includes ≥10 axillary, infraclavicular, supraclavicular, or IM+axillary combination.

7
Forgetting Micrometastasis Category

N1mi (micrometastasis >0.2-2mm) is a distinct category with different prognostic implications than N1.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor

Locate and characterize the primary breast lesion. Distinguish invasive from in-situ components.

2
Measure Invasive Component

Measure maximum dimension of invasive carcinoma only. Document DCIS extent separately.

3
Assess for T4 Features

Systematically evaluate for chest wall invasion, skin involvement, and inflammatory changes.

4
Evaluate Axillary Nodes

Count and measure axillary lymph nodes. Note morphology and enhancement patterns.

5
Check Internal Mammary Chain

Systematically evaluate internal mammary nodes. Measure short axis of any enlarged nodes.

6
Assess Other Nodal Stations

Evaluate supraclavicular, infraclavicular, and contralateral nodal regions.

7
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate for distant metastatic disease in bones, liver, lungs, and other organs as clinically indicated.

8
Synthesize and Stage

Combine T, N, M findings to determine overall stage. Consider prognostic factors and treatment implications.

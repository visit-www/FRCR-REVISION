T Stage Size Cutoffs

T Stage Size Range Description
Tis - Carcinoma in situ (DCIS, LCIS, Paget without invasion)
T1mi ≤1 mm Microinvasion
T1a >1 mm to ≤5 mm Small invasive tumor
T1b >5 mm to ≤10 mm Small invasive tumor
T1c >10 mm to ≤20 mm Small invasive tumor
T2 >20 mm to ≤50 mm Medium tumor
T3 >50 mm Large tumor
T4 Any size Extension to chest wall or skin

N Stage Classification

N Stage Description Key Points
N0 No regional lymph node metastases Includes isolated tumor cells (≤0.2mm)
N1mi Micrometastases (>0.2mm to ≤2mm) Affects prognostic staging
N1 1-3 axillary nodes OR IM sentinel node only IM must be sentinel node biopsy detected
N2 4-9 axillary OR clinically detected IM without axillary Clinically detected = imaging or physical exam
N3 ≥10 axillary OR infraclavicular OR supraclavicular OR IM+axillary Any supraclavicular involvement = N3

Key Imaging Tips

Chest Wall vs Pectoralis

T4a requires invasion of ribs, intercostal muscles, or serratus anterior

Pectoralis muscle invasion alone is NOT T4a. Look for rib cortical irregularity or intercostal muscle involvement.

Skin Involvement Assessment

Distinguish between reactive changes and tumor involvement

T4b requires ulceration, satellite nodules, or edema. Reactive skin thickening from large tumors is not T4b.

Inflammatory Breast Cancer

Clinical diagnosis with rapid onset of erythema and edema

Imaging shows skin thickening, trabecular thickening. Must have clinical signs - imaging alone insufficient.

Internal Mammary Nodes

Significant if >5mm short axis or morphologically abnormal

Located in intercostal spaces 1-3. Distinguish between sentinel node detection vs clinical detection for staging.

Multifocal vs Multicentric

Use largest invasive focus for T staging

Multifocal = same quadrant. Multicentric = different quadrants. Both use largest focus for T stage.

DCIS Extent Measurement

Measure invasive component only for T staging

DCIS extent doesn't affect T stage. Only measure the invasive carcinoma component for staging purposes.

Axillary Node Assessment

Morphologic criteria more important than size

Cortical thickening >3mm, loss of fatty hilum, round shape more predictive than size alone.

Supraclavicular Nodes

Any involvement automatically makes it N3

Located above clavicle, medial to scalene muscles. Even small nodes here significantly worsen prognosis.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Measuring DCIS Component for T Staging

Only the invasive component determines T stage. DCIS extent is reported separately but doesn't affect TNM staging.

2
Calling Pectoralis Invasion T4a

Pectoralis muscle invasion alone is NOT T4a. T4a requires chest wall (ribs, intercostal muscles) invasion.

3
Missing Internal Mammary Nodes

IM nodes significantly affect staging. Look carefully in intercostal spaces 1-3, especially on contrast-enhanced studies.

4
Confusing Reactive vs Inflammatory Changes

Large tumors can cause reactive skin thickening. True inflammatory breast cancer requires clinical correlation.

5
Ignoring Micrometastases

Micrometastases (>0.2-2mm) affect prognostic staging even though they're not counted in anatomic N staging.

6
Incorrect Node Level Assignment

Infraclavicular and supraclavicular nodes are automatically N3, regardless of number or size.

7
Using Only Anatomic Staging

Prognostic staging incorporates biomarkers and grade, providing more accurate prognosis than anatomic staging alone.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Tumor

Locate the primary tumor and distinguish invasive from in-situ components. Measure only the invasive component.

2
Assess T4 Features First

Look for chest wall invasion, skin involvement, or inflammatory changes. These override size-based staging.

3
Measure Maximum Dimension

Use the largest invasive focus if multifocal. Measure on any imaging plane - axial, sagittal, or coronal.

4
Systematic Nodal Assessment

Examine all nodal stations: axillary (levels I-III), internal mammary, infraclavicular, supraclavicular.

5
Count Suspicious Nodes

Use morphologic criteria (cortical thickening, loss of fatty hilum) rather than size alone for node assessment.

6
Screen for Distant Metastases

Examine bones, liver, lungs, and other organs within the field of view for metastatic disease.

7
Correlate with Pathology

Review available pathology for grade, ER/PR/HER2 status, and exact nodal counts for prognostic staging.

8
Determine Both Staging Systems

Calculate anatomic stage (TNM only) and prognostic stage (includes biomarkers) when pathology available.

Prognostic Staging Considerations

The AJCC 9th edition includes prognostic staging that incorporates biomarkers and grade:

Estrogen Receptor (ER)

Positive: ≥1% nuclear staining

Improves prognosis, enables hormonal therapy

Progesterone Receptor (PR)

Positive: ≥1% nuclear staining

Further refines hormonal therapy benefit

HER2

Positive: IHC 3+ or FISH amplified

Enables targeted therapy, affects prognosis

Histologic Grade

Nottingham grade 1-3

Higher grade = worse prognosis

Note: For ER+/HER2- tumors, genomic tests like Oncotype DX may further refine prognosis and treatment decisions, particularly for intermediate-risk cases.

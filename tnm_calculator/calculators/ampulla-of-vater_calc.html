Key Imaging Tips

Arterial Assessment

Evaluate celiac axis, SMA, and common hepatic artery for encasement or narrowing.

Use arterial phase CT/MRI. Look for loss of fat plane, vessel irregularity, or >180° contact.

Pancreatic Invasion Depth

Measure depth of pancreatic invasion carefully - 0.5 cm is the T3a/T3b cutoff.

Use multiplanar reformats. Measure perpendicular to pancreatic surface from deepest point of invasion.

Duodenal Wall Layers

Identify duodenal wall layers to distinguish T1b (submucosa) from T2 (muscularis propria).

High-resolution T2-weighted MRI or thin-section CT can show wall layer detail.

Sphincter of Oddi Recognition

The sphincter of Oddi is thinner and less organized than duodenal muscularis propria.

Look for the delicate muscle fibers surrounding the ampulla - different from thick duodenal muscle.

Peripancreatic Fat Assessment

Evaluate peripancreatic and periduodenal fat for tumor extension (T3b feature).

Look for fat stranding, soft tissue density, or loss of normal fat planes around pancreas/duodenum.

Regional Node Stations

Know regional node stations: pancreaticoduodenal, pyloric, hepatic, superior mesenteric.

Nodes >1 cm short axis are suspicious. Count all positive nodes for N1 vs N2 distinction.

Biliary Obstruction Effects

Ampullary tumors commonly cause biliary and pancreatic duct obstruction.

Look for "double duct sign" - both bile and pancreatic duct dilation. May help localize small tumors.

Duodenal Serosa Identification

Identify duodenal serosa involvement for T3b staging.

Serosa is the outermost layer. Look for tumor reaching the peritoneal surface of the duodenum.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Ampullary vs Duodenal Primary

Ampullary tumors arise at/near the papilla of Vater, while duodenal tumors arise elsewhere in duodenal wall. Location and relationship to ampulla are key distinguishing features.

2
Misinterpreting Pancreatic Invasion Depth

The 0.5 cm cutoff for T3a vs T3b is critical. Always measure the deepest point of pancreatic invasion perpendicular to the pancreatic surface, not along tumor axis.

3
Missing Arterial Involvement

T4 staging requires careful evaluation of celiac axis, SMA, and common hepatic artery. Use arterial phase imaging and look for >180° vessel contact or encasement.

4
Incorrect Node Counting

N1 vs N2 depends on total number of positive nodes (1-3 vs ≥4), not size or location. Count all positive regional nodes carefully.

5
Overlooking Perisphincteric Invasion

T1b includes invasion beyond the sphincter of Oddi (perisphincteric invasion). This is different from and less extensive than duodenal muscularis propria invasion (T2).

6
Confusing Inflammation with Tumor Extension

Obstructive changes and inflammation can mimic tumor extension. Look for definite soft tissue density and architectural distortion, not just fat stranding.

7
Misidentifying Duodenal Wall Layers

Distinguishing submucosa (T1b) from muscularis propria (T2) requires high-resolution imaging. Use thin-section CT or high-resolution MRI to identify wall layers.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify the Primary Tumor

Locate the ampulla of Vater and confirm tumor origin. Look for relationship to papilla and bile/pancreatic duct confluence.

2
Assess Arterial Involvement (T4)

Evaluate celiac axis, SMA, and common hepatic artery first. Any involvement = T4, regardless of other features.

3
Evaluate Pancreatic Invasion

Measure depth of pancreatic invasion. ≤0.5 cm = T3a, >0.5 cm = T3b. Use multiplanar reformats for accurate measurement.

4
Check for T3b Features

Look for peripancreatic tissue, periduodenal tissue, or duodenal serosa involvement. Any of these = T3b.

5
Assess Duodenal Wall Invasion

Determine depth of duodenal wall invasion: submucosa = T1b, muscularis propria = T2. Use high-resolution imaging.

6
Evaluate Sphincter Extension

Check for perisphincteric invasion (beyond sphincter of Oddi). This indicates T1b if no other features present.

7
Count Regional Lymph Nodes

Identify and count all positive regional nodes. 1-3 nodes = N1, ≥4 nodes = N2. Include pancreaticoduodenal, pyloric, hepatic, and superior mesenteric nodes.

8
Screen for Distant Metastases

Evaluate liver, peritoneum, lungs, and other distant sites. Any distant metastasis = M1 and Stage IV disease.

🔍 Key Imaging Tips

Size Measurement

Measure the largest dimension on any imaging plane (axial, coronal, or sagittal).

Use soft tissue windows for accurate measurement. Include any solid components but exclude cystic areas.

Vascular Invasion Assessment

Look for tumor thrombus in renal vein or IVC on contrast-enhanced phases.

Arterial and venous phases are crucial. Look for filling defects, vessel expansion, or enhancement within the vessel lumen.

Adjacent Organ Invasion

Assess for loss of fat planes and direct tumor extension into adjacent structures.

Complete loss of fat plane + irregular interface + enhancement in adjacent organ suggests invasion.

Regional Lymph Nodes

Evaluate para-aortic, paracaval, and renal hilar lymph nodes for enlargement or abnormal enhancement.

Size threshold >1 cm short axis, but also consider morphology, enhancement pattern, and clinical context.

Metastatic Disease

Common sites include lungs, liver, bones, and peritoneum. Always include chest imaging.

ACC has propensity for lung and liver metastases. Bone metastases are typically lytic.

Imaging Protocol

Use dedicated adrenal protocol with pre-contrast, arterial, venous, and delayed phases.

Thin-section CT (≤3mm) with IV contrast. MRI with chemical shift imaging can help characterize lesions.

Functional Assessment

Consider biochemical correlation for hormone-secreting tumors affecting staging implications.

Functional status may influence treatment decisions but doesn't directly affect TNM staging.

Follow-up Imaging

Post-treatment surveillance should assess for local recurrence and distant metastases.

High recurrence rate necessitates long-term follow-up with cross-sectional imaging every 3-6 months initially.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing T3 and T4

T3 is local invasion without adjacent organ involvement, while T4 requires invasion of specific adjacent organs (kidney, diaphragm, pancreas, spleen, liver) or major vessels. Don't upstage T3 to T4 for minor capsular extension.

2
Missing Vascular Invasion

Renal vein or IVC invasion automatically makes it T4, regardless of size. Always carefully evaluate venous phases for tumor thrombus or vessel wall invasion.

3
Inadequate Nodal Assessment

Regional nodes include para-aortic, paracaval, and renal hilar nodes. Don't forget to assess all regional nodal stations, not just the most obvious ones.

4
Size Measurement Errors

Measure the largest dimension on any plane, not just axial. Include solid components only and use consistent measurement techniques across follow-up studies.

5
Overlooking Distant Metastases

Always include chest imaging in staging workup. ACC commonly metastasizes to lungs and liver. Small lung nodules may be the only sign of M1 disease.

6
Misinterpreting Stage III vs IV

Stage IV is reserved exclusively for distant metastatic disease (M1). T4 lesions without distant metastases are Stage III, not Stage IV.

7
Inadequate Contrast Timing

Proper arterial and venous phase timing is crucial for assessing vascular invasion and organ involvement. Poor contrast timing can lead to understaging.

📋 8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Confirm Adrenal Location

Verify the mass is arising from the adrenal gland and not from adjacent structures like kidney or retroperitoneum.

2
Measure Maximum Dimension

Measure the largest dimension on any imaging plane. Document size for T1 (≤5cm) vs T2 (>5cm) determination.

3
Assess Capsular Integrity

Look for capsular disruption or extension beyond the adrenal gland. This determines T3 vs T1/T2 staging.

4
Evaluate Adjacent Organs

Systematically assess kidney, diaphragm, pancreas, spleen, and liver for direct tumor invasion (T4 criteria).

5
Check Vascular Invasion

Carefully examine renal vein and IVC on venous phase images for tumor thrombus or wall invasion (T4 criteria).

6
Survey Regional Nodes

Evaluate para-aortic, paracaval, and renal hilar lymph nodes for size, morphology, and enhancement pattern.

7
Search for Distant Metastases

Systematically review lungs, liver, bones, and peritoneum for metastatic disease. Include chest imaging in staging.

8
Correlate with Clinical Data

Consider functional status, biochemical markers, and clinical presentation to provide comprehensive staging assessment.

📝 Key Changes in AJCC 8th Edition

Important Updates

The AJCC 8th Edition made significant changes to adrenal cortical carcinoma staging based on validation studies:

  • T4 Redefinition: Now includes large vessel invasion (renal vein or vena cava) in addition to adjacent organ invasion
  • Stage IV Restriction: Stage IV is now reserved exclusively for distant metastatic disease (M1)
  • Stage III Expansion: T3 and T4 lesions without distant metastases are now Stage III regardless of nodal status
  • Validation Basis: Changes based on large studies from German ACC Registry and SEER database
  • Improved Discrimination: Better separation between Stage II and Stage III disease outcomes

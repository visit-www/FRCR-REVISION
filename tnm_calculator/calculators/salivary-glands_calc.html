N0 0 nodes N/A Same for both

Key Difference: Salivary gland N staging eliminates N2 subcategories and N3 entirely. Uses simplified system based on node count (≤3 vs >3) and ENE status.

Key Imaging Tips

Extraparenchymal Extension

Critical for T3 staging in major salivary glands

Look for tumor extending beyond the gland capsule into adjacent fat planes or tissues. This is particularly important in parotid tumors where extension beyond the capsule automatically makes it T3 regardless of size.

Facial Nerve Assessment

Facial nerve involvement = automatic T4a

Look for tumor tracking along the facial nerve course, facial nerve enhancement, or clinical facial weakness. MRI with contrast is best for evaluating perineural spread, especially in adenoid cystic carcinoma.

Parotid Deep Lobe Assessment

Deep lobe involvement affects surgical planning

Identify the retromandibular vein as a landmark separating superficial and deep lobes. Deep lobe tumors may extend toward the parapharyngeal space and require more extensive surgery.

Submandibular Gland Anatomy

Understand the mylohyoid muscle relationship

The submandibular gland wraps around the mylohyoid muscle. The deep portion lies above the muscle in the sublingual space, while the superficial portion lies below in the submandibular space.

Minor Salivary Gland Tumors

Most commonly in oral cavity and sinonasal tract

Minor salivary gland tumors can arise anywhere in the upper aerodigestive tract. They follow the same staging as major salivary glands. Look for submucosal masses with characteristic imaging features.

Perineural Invasion Detection

High-resolution MRI with contrast is essential

Look for nerve thickening, enhancement, or loss of normal fat around nerves. Adenoid cystic carcinoma has high propensity for perineural spread. Trace nerves from skull base to periphery.

ENE Assessment in Nodes

Look for capsular breakthrough

ENE appears as irregular nodal margins, stranding into adjacent fat, or loss of normal nodal capsule. CT may show rim enhancement. Size alone is not sufficient for ENE diagnosis.

Mandible Invasion Assessment

Cortical destruction vs pressure erosion

True invasion shows cortical destruction with marrow involvement. Pressure erosion from large tumors may cause smooth cortical thinning but doesn't constitute T4a invasion.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1
Confusing Pleomorphic Adenoma with Malignancy

Pleomorphic adenomas can be large and heterogeneous. Look for capsular integrity, lack of invasion, and characteristic T2 hyperintensity. Malignant transformation shows capsular disruption and invasion.

2
Missing Extraparenchymal Extension

Subtle extension beyond gland capsule can be missed on CT. Use MRI when possible for better soft tissue contrast. Look carefully at fat planes around the gland.

3
Overlooking Perineural Spread

Adenoid cystic carcinoma commonly spreads along nerves, sometimes far from the primary. Always trace the facial nerve and other cranial nerves on MRI with contrast.

4
Misclassifying Lymph Node ENE

Not all irregular node margins represent ENE. True ENE requires definitive capsular breakthrough with extension into adjacent tissues. Reactive changes can mimic ENE.

5
Incorrect Size Measurement

Always measure the largest dimension in any plane, not just axial. Coronal and sagittal measurements may be larger. Include any extraparenchymal extension in size measurement.

6
Forgetting Bilateral Node Assessment

While rare in salivary gland cancers, bilateral nodal involvement can occur. Always assess both sides of the neck, especially for minor salivary gland tumors near midline structures.

7
Misunderstanding N2 Criteria

Salivary gland N staging is simplified compared to other head and neck cancers. N2 is defined as >3 nodes OR any ENE, not the complex size-based criteria used elsewhere in head and neck.

8-Step Systematic Reading Approach

1
Identify Primary Location

Determine if tumor arises from parotid, submandibular, sublingual, or minor salivary glands. This affects surgical approach and staging considerations.

2
Measure Maximum Dimension

Measure largest tumor dimension in any plane (axial, coronal, or sagittal). Include any extraparenchymal extension in the measurement.

3
Assess Extraparenchymal Extension

Look for tumor extension beyond the gland capsule into adjacent fat or tissues. This is critical for T3 staging regardless of size.

4
Evaluate for T4a Features

Systematically check for skin, bone, cartilage, facial nerve, or other adjacent structure invasion. Any T4a feature makes the tumor at least T4a.

5
Screen for T4b Features

Look for skull base, pterygoid plates, carotid encasement, prevertebral space, or other unresectable structure involvement.

6
Count and Measure Lymph Nodes

Count all pathologically enlarged nodes and measure the largest. Remember the simplified N staging: ≤3 nodes vs >3 nodes.

7
Assess for Extranodal Extension

Look for irregular nodal margins, capsular breakthrough, and extension into adjacent tissues. ENE automatically makes it N2.

8
Search for Distant Metastases

Evaluate lungs, liver, bones, and other distant sites. Consider PET-CT for high-grade tumors or when distant disease is suspected.

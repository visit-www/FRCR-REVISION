Leukemia Quick Reference

Radiology Reporting & MDT Discussion — CT / MRI / Staging

No TNM Staging for Leukemia. There is no T, N, or M category and no AJCC prognostic stage group. Leukemia is staged by cytogenetics, molecular markers, and disease-specific classification systems — not by anatomic extent on imaging.

Role of Imaging in Leukemia

What to Report (CT / MRI)

  • Lymphadenopathy — location, size, distribution
  • Hepatosplenomegaly — measure craniocaudal length
  • Bone marrow signal (MRI) — diffuse T1 low signal
  • Extramedullary disease — chloromas / granulocytic sarcomas
  • CNS involvement — leptomeningeal enhancement, cranial nerve thickening
  • Testicular infiltration — in paediatric ALL
  • Complications — infection, haemorrhage, infarction
  • Treatment response — interval change in bulk disease

Common Pitfalls

  • Do not attempt TNM staging — it doesn't exist for leukemia
  • Enlarged nodes ≠ "N stage" — report descriptively
  • Don't confuse reactive marrow (post-chemo / G-CSF) with residual disease
  • Splenic size can be treatment-related, not just disease
  • Chloromas can mimic lymphoma or other solid tumours
  • CNS disease may be occult on imaging — LP is gold standard

General AJCC Rules — Useful for MDT

Staging Time Windows

Clinical StageWithin 4 months of diagnosis, before treatment
Pathological StageWithin 4 months + surgical data (if no neoadjuvant Rx)
yc — Post-neoadjuvant clinicalAfter neoadjuvant therapy, before surgery
yp — Post-neoadjuvant pathAfter neoadjuvant therapy + surgery

Key Rules to Remember

  • Uncertainty rule: assign the lower T/N/M category if in doubt
  • Progression: only pre-progression data counts for staging
  • Synchronous tumours (same organ): stage highest T, add (m) suffix
  • Paired organs: stage each side separately (breast, lung, kidney)
  • Unknown primary (T0): stage based on clinical suspicion of organ

MDT Discussion Points

  • Disease bulk and distribution — localised vs widespread
  • Interval change from prior imaging — response assessment
  • Evidence of extramedullary relapse (chloromas, CNS, testicular)
  • Treatment complications — opportunistic infection, drug toxicity, thrombosis
  • Suitability for stem cell transplant — general fitness, organ assessment
  • Cytogenetics & molecular correlation — imaging doesn't stage leukemia, genetics do
  • Is further imaging needed? (PET for chloromas, MRI brain for CNS)

Memory Aids

🧒

Childhood ALL — Good

High hyperdiploidy (30%) and t(12;21) (25%) = majority are favourable. Think "more is more" — more chromosomes = better.

🧑

Adult ALL — Worse

Ph+ t(9;22) up to 28% — the single most important adverse marker. "Philadelphia = Failure" (pre-TKI). Ask haem about TKI eligibility.

📐

No TNM for Leukemia

"Leukemia Lacks Lumps" — it's a systemic disease. No T, N, M. Report findings descriptively and leave staging to cytogenetics.

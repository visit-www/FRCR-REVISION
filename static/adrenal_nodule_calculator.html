Definition — Adrenal Incidentaloma

  • Incidental adrenal mass >10 mm detected on imaging not performed for suspected adrenal disease
  • No prior history of cancer
  • Non-functional (no clinical signs of hormone excess)

Common Pitfalls

  • Threshold confusion: 10 HU threshold is for unenhanced CT only. Enhanced CT values are not diagnostic.
  • Washout timing: Delayed phase must be at 15 minutes post-contrast. Earlier timing invalidates calculations.
  • Washout mimics: Metastases from RCC/HCC, pheochromocytomas, and ACC may show adenoma-like washout (≥60% absolute).
  • Heterogeneous lesions: Density measurements unreliable in lesions with hemorrhage, necrosis, or cystic components.
  • Size measurement: Use largest axial diameter. Small measurement errors can change management in borderline cases.
  • Biopsy contraindication: Never biopsy suspected pheochromocytoma. Consider biochemical workup first in appropriate clinical context.
  • Bilateral lesions: Consider genetic syndromes (MEN2, VHL) and endocrine evaluation, especially in younger patients.
  • Chemical shift limitation: MRI chemical shift is less sensitive than washout CT when unenhanced density ≥20 HU.

Key Clinical Points

  • Adrenal incidentalomas found in 3% of abdominal CTs, up to 10% in elderly
  • 70% of adenomas are lipid-rich (≤10 HU on unenhanced CT; sensitivity 70-79%, specificity 96-98%)
  • 30% of adenomas are lipid-poor and require washout analysis or MRI
  • Adenoma washout criteria: absolute ≥60% OR relative ≥40%
  • MRI chemical shift: ASR <0.71 or SI-index >16.5% suggests adenoma
  • Size ≥4 cm increases adrenocortical carcinoma risk — ACC typically >6 cm, bulky, irregular with necrosis
  • Pheochromocytoma: T2 "light bulb sign" in 65%; calcifications in 10%; never biopsy without biochemical workup
  • Myelolipoma: macroscopic fat diagnostic; resect if >7 cm, symptomatic, or complicated by bleeding
  • PET-CT: sensitivity 100%, specificity 87-97% for malignancy (SUVmax cutoff 2.68-3.7)
  • All incidentalomas warrant biochemical screening (dexamethasone suppression + plasma metanephrines)
